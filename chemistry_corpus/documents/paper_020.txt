ABSTRACT
Porphyrinic metal-organic frameworks (PMOFs) have
the capacity
to incorporate metalloporphyrins (MPs) into their structure. While
this ability opens the door to many potential applications, the synthesis
of metal-incorporated PMOFs typically involves multistep protocols,
which have so far limited their exploration and scope. Providing a
swift and universal synthesis route is an essential part of developing
these porous materials for catalytic applications. Herein, Zr-PCN-222
and a broad series of its metalated derivatives (Pd, Zn, Ni, Cu, and
Pb) were prepared by adopting a facile and rapid method based on a
three-step microwave-assisted procedure. A variety of diffraction
and spectroscopic techniques were used to characterize the PMOF compounds
and confirm the incorporation of MP moieties into the frameworks,
while their photocatalytic performance was systematically assessed
in light-driven hydrogen evolution reaction (HER) experiments. We
demonstrate that the highest performing Zr-PCN-222­(Pd) analogue exhibits
HER activity under both UV- and visible-light illumination with activity
levels comparable to those of previously reported benchmarks prepared
using more tedious preparation protocols. Our results pave the way
toward the exploration of a range of metal-incorporated PMOFs in applications
where efficient charge separation and distinct optoelectronic properties
are advantageous.

INTRODUCTION
Metal-organic frameworks
(MOFs) are a multifunctional group
of porous compounds composed of metal clusters/ions linked to polydentate
organic linkers, resulting in a well-defined, crystalline structure. - Due to their chemical versatility, high surface areas, rigid skeletons,
and tunable porosity, they have aroused widespread interest during
the past decades, which makes them highly promising for a wide range
of applications, including (photo)­catalysis, gas storage, separation,
sensing, and energy conversion. -

Highly symmetrical porphyrin ligands are naturally well known
as
large heterocyclic molecules. They typically contain four pyrrole
subunits that can coordinate with metal ions (Fe, Zn, Cu, Ni, Mn,
or other transition metals) to form metalloporphyrins (MPs). Porphyrinic MOFs (PMOFs) are a substantial subclass
of MOFs built by incorporating MPs into their backbones to form a
porous, crystalline network. By leveraging
the useful redox-active and light emission/absorption properties of
MPs, PMOFs have gained attention in energy harvesting, photocatalysis,
and sensing.

The photocatalytic
hydrogen evolution reaction (HER) involves a
process where hydrogen (H 2 ) is generated from H 2 O by employing a photocatalyst activated upon absorption of UV or
visible light photons. , This process is seen as an extremely
sustainable way of green H 2 production, which has influenced
research on sustainability and solar fuel technologies over the past
decades. , Among PMOFs, zirconium-based PMOFs (Zr-PMOFs)
exhibit great thermal and chemical durability in comparison with other
MOFs which often decompose under harsh reaction conditions, particularly
in water and acidic or basic media. Porous
coordination network (PCN), namely, PCN-222, is a part of the Zr-PMOFs
family , and is built of meso-tetrakis­(4-carboxyphenyl)­porphyrin
(TCPP) building units allowing for relatively large pores (3.7 nm) and adjustable pore environments. The presence
of abundant coordination sites within the TCPP moieties of PCN-222
further provides an efficient synthetic platform for active catalytic
site incorporation and manipulation. - Apart from having this
role, the conjugated structure of TCPP is able to harness light efficiently
within the photocatalytic process due to respective Soret and Q-absorption
bands and shows a considerably longer lifetime of electron-hole
photorecombination. , This unique property of porphyrinic
units (acting as photosensitizers) leads to the generation of excited
electrons and their transfer to a coordinated metal center (acting
as catalytic sites), giving rise to efficient photocatalytic H 2 generation. ,

Several studies have been
executed to explore the photocatalytic
performance of PMOFs , - with a few works reporting their light-driven HER activity. The additional use of the porphyritic moiety
to incorporate HER-active cocatalysts seems especially attractive
for this application. Such metalated PMOFs allow us to explore the
concept of single-atom catalysis, enable high structural control over
the location and loading of the cocatalyst sites, and result in the
proximity of light-absorbing and catalytic units, which is important
to facilitate charge separation. The first demonstration of photocatalytic
H 2 production under visible-light irradiation using PCN-222
was, however, only realized in 2021 by incorporating different ratios
of TCPP and TCPP­(Pt) into the framework. Follow-up works focused on the development of more complex composites
and heterojunctions; - however, only little effort was dedicated to the optimization of
the preparation protocols toward metalated PMOFs and their derivatives.
As such, a series of Pt-loaded PCN-222­(Pt) was prepared using commercially
available but expensive premetalized TCPP­(Pt) as a precursor. In further works, single-atom-loaded PCN-222-NH 2 (Pd) was fabricated using TCPP­(Pd) as a starting material, and a series of metalated CdS@PCN-222 composites
was synthesized from corresponding MPs. Most recently, different transition-metal ions have been incorporated
into PCN-222 by presynthesizing a range of TCPP­(M) complexes. One problematic issue with this approach is
that the scope of commercially available TCPP­(M) MPs is rather limited,
while typical procedures for PCN-222 metalation involve a long period
of preparation including the fabrication of TPP-COOMe, its metalation,
and hydrolysis to produce TCPP­(M). Often, these protocols demand the
use of high temperatures and deleterious solvents while usually suffering
from low product yields. , ,

The focus of our work lies in the implementation of a rapid,
microwave-based
synthesis approach that makes metalated PMOFs readily available and
facilitates their exploration, which we showcase here using the example
of heterogeneous photocatalysis. Keeping these viewpoints in mind,
in this contribution, we adopt a facile synthetic method where a three-step
microwave-assisted procedure is used to generate a range of metalated
PCN-222. We demonstrate the use of this
PCN-222­(M) series (where M = Pd, Zn, Ni, Cu, and Pb) in light-driven
H 2 generation and show that the PCN-222­(Pd) member displays
significant HER activity under both UV- and visible-light illumination,
with an activity level similar to that of previously reported benchmark
PMOFs. The results demonstrate that metal incorporation significantly
influences photocatalytic performance, while postcatalytic characterization
reveals that framework stability represents a critical factor governing
long-term activity.

This universal approach can be used for
the synthesis of numerous
metalated PMOFs and opens the door to diverse applications specifically
in (photo)­catalysis, gas sorption, biomedicine, and energy storage
materials. Previous studies have demonstrated the potential of metalated
porphyrinic MOFs for photocatalytic hydrogen evolution. The objective
of the present work is not to introduce a fundamentally new MOF topology
but rather to provide a comparative assessment of different metal
incorporations within the PCN-222 platform using a unified synthetic
and photocatalytic framework. Consequently,
in contrast to earlier attributed research works on porphyrinic MOFs
for HER, this work provides a systematic
comparison of multiple metalated PCN-222 materials (Pd, Ni, Zn, Cu,
and Pb) synthesized under identical conditions. Particular emphasis
is placed on correlating metal incorporation efficiency, photocatalytic
activity, and reproducibility, which are important parameters for
evaluating the practical applicability of these materials, while also
highlighting the role of postcatalytic structural stability under
photocatalytic conditions.

RESULTS AND DISCUSSION
The preparation of metal-free
PCN-222 and its metalated derivatives was accomplished based on a
modified procedure reported previously. The synthesis pathways and activation process are discussed thoroughly
in the Electronic Supporting Information (ESI, Section S2 ). Briefly, the synthesis proceeds
in three rapid microwave-assisted steps ( Scheme a) in which the first and second steps yield
Zr nodes (secondary building units (SBUs)) and MPs, respectively,
by adding ZrOCl 2 to 2-fluorobenzoic acid (2FBA) (ratio
1:25) and pouring MCl 2 to TCCP (ratio 5:1), both in N , N -dimethylformamide (DMF). The respective
solutions only require 5 min of heating at 140 and 175 °C, respectively,
to complete the reaction. The third step, which forms PCN-222­(M),
is conducted by mixing the as-gained suspensions of the first and
the second steps followed by the addition of trifluoroacetic acid
(TFA) in DMF; the resulting suspension is heated at 150 °C for
30 min to yield the desired PCN-222­(M).

Photocatalytic
HER studies were performed by dispersing the catalyst powders in acetonitrile/water
mixtures and using triethylamine (TEOA) as an electron scavenger (details
in ESI, Section S3 ). The HER rates of PCN-222
(M) are summarized in Figure . PCN-222­(Zn, Ni, Pb) showed low but measurable H 2 production after 3 h of light irradiation with a narrow-band UV
source (365 nm), yielding 2.5, 0.5, and 2.9 μmol g -1 of H 2 , respectively ( Figure a). While this low level of activity for
PCN-222­(Zn) and PCN-222­(Pb) can be linked to the low incorporation
degree of these metals, the performance of PCN-222­(Ni), where the
metalation was successful, is likely an indicator of its intrinsically
low activity. In contrast, nonmetalated PCN-222 and PCN-222­(Cu) generated
no measurable H 2 ( Table S2 ),
which can be related to the fast recombination of photogenerated electron-hole
pairs in the parent PCN-222 , and the inability of
Cu to cocatalyze HER under these conditions, respectively.

In comparison to the rest of the compounds, PCN-222­(Pd)
displayed
by far the highest HER rate generating 120.5 μmol g -1 of H 2 after 3 h of illumination suggesting that the photoelectrons
excited on the porphyrin moiety are able to effectively migrate to
the Pd site and take part in the proton reduction reaction generating
dihydrogen. This activity also goes in line with the high degree of
Pd incorporation, highlighting the intrinsic affinity of the TCPP
moiety to coordinate Pd 2+ (d 8 ) in a square planar
geometry. The H 2 generation rates of all studied PCN-222­(Pd)
photocatalysts are provided in Figure b and further summarized in Table S3 . While no substantial photocatalytic activities employing
narrow-band visible light illumination (445 nm) were witnessed with
neat PCN-222 or other metalated PCN-222­(M) derivatives, PCN-222­(Pd)
also yielded a substantial HER rate (24.1 μmol g -1 ) which was only 3-5 times lower compared to the rate obtained
under UV illumination ( Figure b). Calculated apparent quantum yield (AQY) values under visible-
and UV-light illumination amount to 0.004 and 0.04%, respectively,
which emphasize the potential of using metalated PMOFs in photocatalytic
conversions driven by natural sunlight.

It is worth noting that
TEOA was used as a sacrificial electron
donor to scavenge photogenerated holes and suppress charge recombination.
Under the applied conditions, TEOA does not produce measurable hydrogen
in the absence of a photocatalyst ( Table S3 ), ensuring that the observed H 2 evolution originates
from photocatalytic processes involving the PCN-222-based materials
rather than from photochemical decomposition of the sacrificial donor.

Steady-state photoluminescence (PL) spectroscopy
was applied to
further rationalize the activity trend of PCN-222 (M). Figure shows that metalation of PCN-222
always leads to strong quenching of its main emissive peaks, whereas
PCN-222­(Pb,Zn) only shows minor effects likely due to the low incorporation
degree of the metals. Considering that both PCN-222­(Pb) and PCN-222­(Zn)
still show HER activity, PL suggests rather efficient charge separation
enabled at the Pb- and Zn-containing porphyrinic moieties.

In contrast to this, PCN-222 (Pd,Ni) both show
very strong quenching
behavior, resulting in 15-30 times lower recombination processes
within their respective frameworks. This property yields an outstanding
HER performance for the Pd analogue; however, contrary to expectations
based on PL and successful metal incorporation, PCN-222­(Ni) is still
quite inactive ( Figure a), which we associate with the inability of Ni 2+ to efficiently
catalyze HER in its single-atom form. In line with this, recent literature
indicates that PCN-222­(Ni) is active toward CO 2 reduction
rather than HER.

A plausible photocatalytic
pathway involves the excitation of the
porphyrinic linker, followed by intersystem crossing and electron
transfer to the metal centers, which act as catalytic sites for proton
reduction. While this mechanism is supported by photoluminescence
quenching and catalytic trends, it should be considered as a plausible
interpretation based on indirect evidence. Photoluminescence measurements probe singlet excited states and
are therefore not directly representative of the triplet states typically
involved in photocatalytic processes. In this work, PL quenching is used as an indirect indicator of excited-state
interactions between the linker and metal centers rather than as direct
evidence of photocatalytic activity. Despite the limitations of PL
spectroscopy for mechanistic assignment, a qualitative comparison
of the PL and photocatalytic results remains informative. Both PCN-222­(Pd)
and PCN-222­(Ni) exhibit substantial PL quenching, suggesting efficient
excited-state interactions. However, their markedly different HER
activities indicate that photocatalytic performance is not governed
solely by charge separation efficiency. Instead, the intrinsic catalytic
properties of the incorporated metal centers play a decisive role
in determining the hydrogen evolution activity.

We further benchmarked
the performance of the best-performing PCN-222­(Pd)
against physical mixtures of PCN-222 and Pd- or Pt-containing precursors
(details in Table S3 ). First, photodeposited
Pd/PCN-222 did not produce any H 2 , which suggests that
the location of the Pd atom is important in the extraction and consumption
of the photogenerated charges. In comparison to Pd nanoparticles deposited
on the surface of PCN-222 particles, single Pd atoms incorporated
inside light-absorbing porphyrinic moieties seem to be able to effectively
use the photogenerated charge and complete the HER. Second, despite
Pt being known as one of the most active HER cocatalysts, Pt/PCN-222 displays much lower photocatalytic
HER rates (13-fold) compared to our PCN-222­(Pd), which further demonstrates
the potential of PMOFs containing suitable catalytic centers in driving
photocatalysis efficiently.

We next assessed both the reproducibility
of the synthesis and
the photocatalytic performance by examining the PCN-222­(Pd) member
of the family. Several batches of PCN-222­(Pd) were prepared on a single
day, and the resulting products were analyzed with PXRD ( Figure S3 ). The diffraction patterns match well
with that of the first PCN-222­(Pd) described above, manifesting the
general reliability of the synthetic process. All three batches were
further tested toward their HER performance, and all were shown to
be highly active with activity values deviating by 20% from the average
( Table S4 ). While this result generally
points to the reproducibility of the catalytic data, this high deviation
value suggest that even small synthetic modifications along the 3-step
synthesis procedure may result in a different degree of Pd atom incorporation
and type. Whereas the aspect of MOF synthesis reproducibility is a
matter of wider research, ,

Under UV irradiation,
the exact photoinduced charge transfer pathway
operating under the present reaction conditions cannot be conclusively
established from the available data. Nevertheless, the observed photocatalytic
activity is consistent with the excitation of the porphyrinic framework
followed by charge separation and electron transfer to catalytically

Apart from testing different
batches, we also evaluated the long-term
performance of the second PCN-222­(Pd) batch under light on-off
cycles simulating day-night cycles (details in Figure c and ESI Section S3 ; Table S4 ). After each
3 h long illumination period, the reactor was opened and purged with
Ar for 10 min; the illumination was continued over the following 3
h period. Regardless of retaining its macroscopic appearance ( Figure S4c ), PXRD and FTIR analyses ( Figure S4a and S4b , respectively) show partial
framework decomposition after photocatalysis, evidenced by linker-like
vibrational features and loss of crystallinity. In particular, peak
broadening and loss of long-range crystallinity in PXRD spectra have
revealed that PCN-222­(Pd) underwent partial structural decomposition
during photocatalytic HER. All of these are indicative of framework
collapse and partial amorphization. Furthermore, the characteristic
carboxylate coordination bands associated with the Zr-porphyrin framework
in the FTIR spectra became broadened and shifted, while new bands
resembling those of the free porphyrinic ligand emerged.

The
gradual decrease in hydrogen evolution activity observed during
successive photocatalytic cycles appears to be closely associated
with structural changes in the catalyst. Postcatalytic FTIR spectra
revealed broadening and shifting of characteristic framework absorption
bands, while PXRD patterns showed a noticeable reduction in diffraction
reflection intensities and partial loss of crystallinity. These observations
suggest partial degradation of the PCN-222­(Pd) framework under photocatalytic
conditions. Since the porphyrinic linker and the coordinated Pd centers
constitute the catalytically active architecture, disruption of the
framework is expected to reduce the accessibility and effectiveness
of active sites, thereby contributing to the progressive decline in
the HER performance.

PCN-222-type frameworks are generally stable
in aqueous media due
to the robustness of Zr-O coordination bonds. , However, under photocatalytic conditions, partial structural degradation
is observed, as evidenced by FTIR and PXRD analyses. This suggests
that while the framework remains stable in the dark, the combination
of irradiation and reactive intermediates generated during the photocatalytic
process leads to a gradual loss of crystallinity and partial linker
dissociation. , Despite the fact that the exact
trend is a matter of further investigation, this result shows that
PCN-222­(Pd) generally exhibits high activity even after 12 h of illumination
and does not show dramatic deactivation commonly observed in other
photocatalytic MOFs. ,

CONCLUSION
In summary, a swift microwave-based
fabrication of metalated PCN-222
was successfully applied to prepare a series of its Pd-, Zn-, Ni-,
Cu-, and Pb-containing derivatives. Several characterization methods
were used for characterization: PXRD revealed the iso-reticular crystalline
character of PCN-222­(M) analogs; FTIR, TXRF, and DRS confirmed successful
metalation. The photocatalytic HER activities of PCN-222­(M) were tested
under UV- and visible-light revealing minor HER activities for PCN-222­(Zn,
Pb, Cu), whereas PCN-222­(Pd) demonstrated high H 2 evolution
rates reaching AQY values of 0.04. Following its strong absorption
in the visible-light region, PCN-222­(Pd) could also deliver substantial
activity under visible-light illumination.

Steady-state PL results
showed that the presence of Pd and Ni centers
inside the porphyrin cores results in efficient charge separation
and leads to a strongly suppressed radiative emission of the PMOF.
While Ni sites stay inactive toward HER in PCN-222­(Ni), this efficient
charge separation and the additional ability to catalytically promote
H 2 generation by Pd turned PCN-222­(Pb) into an active photocatalyst.
It should be noted that steady-state photoluminescence measurements
predominantly probe processes originating from singlet excited states
and therefore do not directly monitor the long-lived triplet states
that are typically involved in photocatalytic hydrogen evolution.
Consequently, PL quenching is interpreted here only as evidence of
excited-state interactions between the porphyrinic linker and incorporated
metal centers. The PL data are not considered direct proof of the
charge-transfer processes responsible for photocatalysis but rather
provide complementary information regarding electronic communication
within the framework.

The robustness of the synthetic protocol
and the photocatalytic
performance of PCN-222­(Pd) were examined by producing multiple catalyst
batches. The reusability of the best-performing PCN-222­(Pd) was tested
by lengthy exposure of the catalyst to light (12 h; four successive
cycles), displaying its sustained HER efficiency. This work advances
the use of metal-incorporated PMOFs and paves the way for their further
exploration. However, the hydrogen evolution rates obtained in this
study are lower than those reported for some optimized porphyrinic
MOF systems. Direct comparison between literature reports remains
challenging because photocatalytic performance is highly dependent
on experimental variables such as the irradiation source, sacrificial
agent, catalyst loading, and reactor configuration. Therefore, the
present work focuses primarily on relative activity trends among the
investigated metalated PCN-222 materials under identical reaction
conditions rather than on achieving maximum hydrogen productivity.

Although the microwave-assisted synthesis of PCN-222-based materials
has been previously reported, the present approach focuses on establishing
a general and reproducible protocol for metal incorporation across
different ions. The emphasis of this work is not on minimizing the
number of synthetic steps but rather on enabling consistent comparison
of metal-dependent properties within a unified synthetic framework.
Furthermore, in the current work, microwave synthesis was employed
as a rapid and reproducible route to prepare a series of metalated
PCN-222 derivatives under comparable conditions. Accordingly, the
focus of this study is placed on comparative catalytic evaluation
and structure-performance relationships rather than on the
development of a fundamentally new synthetic methodology.

While
the photocatalytic efficiencies remain moderate and the exact
charge-transfer mechanism requires further investigation using advanced
time-resolved spectroscopic techniques, the present study provides
useful insights into the interplay among metal identity, framework
integrity, and photocatalytic hydrogen evolution in porphyrinic MOFs.
Future research lines will focus on further optimization of catalyst
loading, light intensity, and reaction conditions that could significantly
improve AQY values, catalytic efficiency, and stability under operating
conditions.
