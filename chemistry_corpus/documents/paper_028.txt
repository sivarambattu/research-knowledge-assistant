ABSTRACT
Conjugated porous polymers are an important class of advanced materials whose precise synthesis is critical for applications in energy storage, photocatalysis, and next-generation electronics. Developing greener routes remains challenging due to solvent-intensive, energy-consuming methods. Ball milling has emerged as a powerful and versatile tool in organic synthesis and materials chemistry, offering a sustainable and solvent-free alternative to traditional solution-phase methods. Here, we report the optimization of a triple Sonogashira cross-coupling reaction under room-temperature ball-milling conditions, achieving high yields in short reaction times of 60 min. The optimal catalytic conditions involved PdCl 2 (PPh 3 ) 2 and CuI as co-catalysts, triethylamine (TEA) organobase, and mechanochemical activation with low frequency (25 Hz) in air. The ball-milling coupling also proceeds with solid bases such as Cs 2 CO 3 and under Pd-free or Cu-free variants, albeit with lower yields. Using triethynylbenzene as the core, ten derivatives incorporating electron-rich and electron-deficient aryl halides were synthesized, demonstrating scope and functional-group tolerance. The methodology extends to synthesis of CPPs, reporting three representative macromolecules comprising electron-neutral, rich, and deficient aromatics in high yields (71-89%). This mechanochemical approach enables a green, solvent-free, and operationally simple route to precisely structured advanced materials. INTRODUCTION
Conjugated porous polymers (CPPs) are attracting increasing attention due to their stability, extended π-conjugation, structural tunability, and high surface areas, which make them attractive candidates in energy storage, gas separation, catalysis, and optoelectronics 1 - 7 . Their stability can be further enhanced by the incorporation of heteroatoms and key functional groups including electron-donating groups such as alkoxy and amino groups and electron-withdrawing groups such as F, Br, and CN. The introduction of such functionalities, however, increases their synthetic complexity 1 , 8 - 11 . Traditional synthesis of CPPs typically relies on metal-catalysed cross-coupling reactions such as Suzuki, Sonogashira, and Yamamoto couplings, which often require prolonged reaction times and elevated temperatures that can be as high as 150 °C. For instance, Jiang et al.. demonstrated such synthesis via a classical solution-phase approach, which required extended reaction times of up to 72 h at 80 °C. Even under these conditions, the yields were moderate (ca. 60%), highlighting the inherent limitations of traditional methods in terms of efficiency and scalability 12 . Moreover, such harsh synthetic conditions can cause catalyst degradation, incomplete polymerization, and poor reproducibility. The additional requirements of an inert atmosphere and the high sensitivity of coupling reactions to oxygen and moisture further complicate the overall synthesis 13 - 15 . To overcome these limitations, researchers are increasingly turning to solvent-free mechanochemical approaches for the synthesis of polymeric networks. In contrast to conventional methods, mechanochemical synthesis eliminates the need for toxic solvents and often proceeds without elevated temperatures or extended reaction times, thereby reducing inert-atmosphere requirements, energy consumption, waste generation, and overall environmental impact 16 - 19 . Mechanochemistry has emerged as an innovative technique across several sectors in materials science, pharmaceuticals, catalysis, and sustainable chemistry 20 - 26 . These reactions utilize high-energy ball milling collisions to accelerate reactions through intensive collisions that enable solvent-free synthetic pathways.

Sonogashira couplings (SC) play a crucial role in materials chemistry by extending conjugation across aromatic systems through alkyne-functionalized compounds, creating rigid frameworks. The presence of alkyne groups also enables post-functionalization of the macromolecules via several reactions, such as azide-alkyne cycloaddition and thiol-yne reactions 27 - 29 . Given its importance, current research focuses on greener, less waste- and energy-intensive automated approaches to improve safety, sustainability and efficiency 30 - 34 . Mechanochemical SC, therefore, offers a promising route for developing novel organic materials tailored to diverse applications 27 , 32 , 35 . The ball milling method for SC was first reported in 2009 by Fulmer and co-workers, who introduced it as a high-speed ball milling (HSBM) technique with a reaction time of 17 h (Fig. 1 A) 36 . This first pioneering report also showed that the coupling of para- substituted aryl halides can proceed when the CuI cocatalyst is replaced with Cu milling balls. Subsequently, Thorwirth et al. developed a copper- and ligand-free ball-milling method with 1,4-diazabicyclo[2,2,2]octane (DABCO) as the organobase (Fig. 1 B) 37 . In 2022, Pickhardt and co-workers reported a mechanochemical SC strategy that employed palladium milling balls as the reaction catalyst (Fig. 1 C) 38 . This strategy, termed “direct mechanocatalysis”, improves catalyst recycling and facilitates separation from the reaction medium 39 . The study investigated the effect of different combinations of DABCO, triphenylphosphine ligand, and the CuI co-catalyst and highlighted the formation of a distinct, air and moisture-stable, co-catalyst complex, different from that generally observed in solution 38 ..

Gao et al.. (Fig. 1 D) reported a copper-free, high-temperature ball-milling approach. At ambient temperature, the reaction produced low yields, indicating that the energy input was insufficient for effective coupling 27 . While external heating from a manually added heat gun increased the yield, direct heating of the milling jars is impractical for sustained operation. Despite the challenging setup, this method enabled improved yields of polyaromatic structures, which serve as key building blocks for luminescent materials and metal-organic frameworks 27 , 40 . Polyaromatic scaffolds exhibit low reactivity under room-temperature ball-milling conditions and pose challenges in standard solution-based synthesis due to their limited solubility 41 . Consequently, the limited number of studies on ball milling SC are primarily focused on monohalogenated substrates rather than polymer synthesis 20 , 34 , 36 , 37 . Polyhalogenated reactants are scarcely explored despite their importance. Multiple Sonogashira couplings on polyhalogenated scaffolds in one pot is not a trivial repetition of coupling steps. It requires active suppression of Glaser homocoupling, whose risk rises with the presence of multiple terminal alkynes, and careful control of kinetically non-equivalent successive couplings 33 , 42 .

Addressing this gap, the present study reports the first successful demonstration of multiple SC via a mechanochemical approach within a short reaction time of 60 min under air and ambient conditions using low frequency (25 Hz). This method enables the efficient synthesis of CPPs. The study systematically explores various experimental parameters, including catalytic systems, bases, and milling ball types, to elucidate the optimal conditions for triple SC between 1,3,5-triethynylbenzene (TEB) and iodobenzene (Fig. 1 E). The scope of the reaction was evaluated, where substrates with electron-donating, electron-withdrawing, and heteroaromatic groups were coupled successfully to TEB, while also assessing the substitution positions. Next, representative conjugated polymeric networks were prepared using electron-neutral, -rich, and -deficient coupling partners.

RESULTS AND DISCUSSION
To investigate the reaction conditions for the triple SC, we selected iodobenzene and TEB as model substrates and screened different reaction conditions, as shown in Table 1 . The initial attempt involved the use of 5 mol% bis(triphenylphosphine)palladium chloride (PdCl 2 (PPh 3 ) 2 ) with 5 mol% copper(I) iodide (CuI) and DABCO as the organobase under air. The reaction was conducted using 7 mm stainless steel (SS) milling balls, and yielded 47% of the desired 1,3,5-tris(phenylethynyl)benzene (TPEB) product within 60 min (Table 1 , Entry 1). Repeating the reaction without Pd and Cu catalysts did not produce TPEB, confirming that SS balls are unsuitable as catalysts and do not contain any trace of catalytically active metals such as Pd or Cu.

Encouraged by this result, we then examined the impact of different bases on the SC reaction, including sodium carbonate (Na 2 CO 3 ), cesium carbonate (Cs 2 CO 3 ), and TEA (Et 3 N) (Table 1 , Entries 2-4). Na 2 CO 3 gave similar results to DABCO but required a longer reaction time, while Cs 2 CO 3 afforded an improved yield of 80%, consistent with its known efficiency in coupling reactions 43 - 46 . The organobase, Et 3 N, exhibited remarkable efficiency, resulting in the highest yield (92%), which can be attributed to its dual functionality during the reaction as a base and as a liquid-assisted grinding (LAG) agent 47 . The LAG parameter, η, defined as the volume of the liquid (Et 3 N base) per total mass of reagents, was calculated to be 0.69 µL mg⁻¹ (SI). The presence of a small amount of liquid helps prevent substrates from forming a sticky paste that clings to the vial walls, a phenomenon known as snowballing, which disrupts mixing and reduces reaction efficiency 48 ..

Next, we evaluated the impact of the catalysts on the efficacy of the SC reaction. The Cu-free conditions (Table 1 , Entry 5) afforded the desired product, albeit in moderate yields (54%). Mechanochemical SC mechanism is poorly understood due to its fast-vibrating nature in a sealed dense vessel that requires advanced in situ characterization setups. However, it is worth noting that in Cu-free solution-based SC reactions, the terminal alkyne is deprotonated by a base to form an alkynyl anion, which transmetalates to the Pd-aryl complex. This approach eliminates the risk of Glaser-type oxidative homocoupling and offers better compatibility with sensitive functional groups 49 , 50 .

The influence of different palladium sources was also assessed, retaining Et 3 N as the base. Palladium acetate (Pd(OAc) 2 ) and tris(dibenzylideneacetone)dipalladium (Pd 2 (dba) 3 ) resulted in significantly lower yields of 30% and 60%, respectively (Table 1 , Entries 6-7). Further trials with tetrakis(triphenylphosphine) palladium(0) (Pd(PPh 3 ) 4 ) afforded TPEB in 82% yield (Table 1 , Entry 8). Among the palladium catalysts evaluated, Pd(PPh 3 ) 2 Cl 2 proved the most effective (Table 1 , Entry 4), with high yields of the desired TPEB product due to its advantageous activation profile and greater operational stability. As a Pd(II) complex, Pd(PPh 3 ) 2 Cl 2 is readily reduced in situ, typically by the amine or the alkyne, to generate the catalytically active 14-electron Pd (0) (PPh 3 ) 2 species, which engages rapidly in oxidative addition to aryl halides. By contrast, ligand dissociation from Pd(PPh 3 ) 4 to produce Pd (0) (PPh 3 ) 2 requires overcoming the enthalpic cost of breaking Pd-P bonds, and under typical reaction conditions Pd (0) (PPh 3 ) 3 is often the resting state; consequently, the buildup of free PPh 3 shifts the pre-equilibrium toward this more highly ligated, less reactive species and can depress initial rates. Pd(PPh 3 ) 2 Cl 2 is also less prone to air- and moisture-induced degradation, unlike Pd(PPh 3 ) 4 , which suffers from phosphine oxidation and ligand loss, reducing operational robustness in sustainable or industrial settings. The halides in the catalyst can additionally influence oxidative-addition kinetics and stabilize on-cycle Pd(II) intermediates (e.g., ArPdI(Cl)(PPh 3 ) 2 ⁻) that facilitate transmetalation with copper acetylides 50 , 51 . While both complexes ultimately generate the same active species under catalytic conditions, the superior stability and more controllable reduction pathway of PdCl₂(PPh₃)₂ underscore its advantage in SC reactions.

We then examined different copper co-catalysts, including CuBr, Cu(OAc) 2 , and CuCl (Table 1 , Entries 9-11). CuI gave a 92% yield, versus 84% for CuCl, while CuBr delivered a lower yield of 51%. Cu(OAc) 2 was the least effective co-catalyst, affording only a 19% yield, suggesting that the counterion plays a key role in catalytic efficiency.

The influence of milling ball material and size was systematically studied. Among the tested materials, stainless steel (SS; 4 mm and 7 mm), zirconium oxide (ZrO 2 ; 5 mm and 10 mm), and titanium carbide (TiC; 4 mm and 7 mm), the 7 mm SS balls consistently delivered the best results (see Table 1 , Entries 4, 11 versus 12-16). This outcome aligns with reports that small changes in the ball-to-jar size ratio or materials can shift the ball-motion regime and thereby alter reaction outcomes, through differences in elasticity, friction, and contact behavior. In our setup, the 7 mm SS media likely provide the most favorable motion regime and contact dynamics for the multiple SC 52 .

The catalytic loading was also assessed, and it was found that decreasing the Cu and Pd sources below 5 mol% (Table 1 , Entries 17-18) produced lower yields of product (~ 60%), although the reaction remained reasonably effective even at 1 mol% of the catalysts. This offers a lower-metal-loading option preferred for some contexts, such as biological applications, albeit at the expense of yield. Lastly, decreasing the amount of Et 3 N to 6 eq. led to a drop in yield to below 50% (Table 1 , Entry 19), further confirming its dual role as base and LAG additive.

The ideal conditions for the triple SC reaction were established to be as follows: PdCl 2 (PPh 3 ) 2 (5 mol%) as the catalyst, CuI (5 mol%) as the co-catalyst, TEA as an organobase, and 7 mm SS milling balls. Under these conditions, the SC reaction proceeded efficiently, yielding 92% of the product (Table 1 , Entry 4) in just 60 min. This systematic evaluation underscores the importance of catalyst selection, base choice, and mechanochemical parameters in maximising the efficiency of solvent-free multiple SC reactions.

With the optimized mechanosynthetic conditions in hand, a range of halogenated aromatics furnished tri-arylated, acetylene-linked conjugates (Fig. 2 ). Aryl iodides bearing electron-neutral, -donating, and -withdrawing groups reacted smoothly under the mechanochemical Sonogashira conditions to give the desired tri-arylated products ( 1-8 ) in moderate to very good yields. 1-Iodonaphthalene afforded the coupled product 1 in 85% yield, which was the highest yield of the series, and is consistent with the optimized TPEP model reaction (92%) using phenyl iodide (Table 1 ). This is likely due to the overall structural stability of the conjugated product, which is devoid of heteroatoms and substituents. The impact of the substituent position relative to the iodo group was assessed using iodomethylbenzene regioisomers. The meta -substituted tri-arylated product 2 was obtained in the highest yield (62%) followed by the para- substituted product 4 at 42% yield. The ortho -substituted aryl iodide afforded the lowest yield for product 3 (28%), which can be attributed to the steric and electronic repulsion effects that hinder the formation of the reaction intermediate with the Pd/Cu catalytic species 42 , 53 - 56 .

We then investigated haloaromatics bearing different electron-donating and -withdrawing substituents. The para-methoxy aryl iodide yielded 5 in 80%, while m-N, N-dimethylaminoiodobenzene afforded 6 in 50%, likely due to amine coordination to the copper co-catalyst that attenuates its activity 57 . Additional experiments with o -iodo- N , N -dimethylaniline and p -iodoaniline (Table S1 , Entries 1-2) did not furnish the desired SC product, supporting the inhibitory effect of aromatic amines under the tested conditions. To probe this hypothesis, the model reaction under optimal conditions was run with a substoichiometric amount (0.5 equiv) of N, N-dimethylaniline; the yield of TPEB decreased from 92% to 49% (Table S1 , Entry 3). The developed mechanosynthetic conditions also exhibited limitations with alkyl iodide (Table S1 , Entry 4) as well as some chlorinated (Table S1 , Entry 5) and brominated aryl substrates (Table S1 , Entries 6-9), even with the addition of KI to promote halogen exchange and oxidative addition. These observations are consistent with previously reported challenges associated with less activated/electrophilic aryl chlorides and bromides, particularly under mechanochemical SC conditions 58 . The aryl iodides bearing electron-accepting groups (cyano and acetyl) reacted well under the current conditions to afford products 7 and 8 in 71% and 52% yields, respectively. These results demonstrate the tolerance of electronic differences within the substrates and the presence of various functional reactive groups, which are suitable for post-functionalization, such as product 7 bearing the cyano group.

Considering the reactivity of heteroaromatic compounds under the tested conditions, the electron-deficient pyridine-containing product 9 was obtained from o -iodopyridine, albeit with a low yield (21%), similar to other amine-containing reactants tested in this study, which have basic and coordinative properties, such as the pyridine ring. The electron-rich 2-iodothiophene, expected to exhibit slower kinetics in the oxidative addition step 42 , did not undergo coupling under the optimized mechanochemical conditions (Table 2 , Entry 1). Similar results were obtained when 2-bromothiophene was subjected to the same reaction conditions, in the presence or absence of KI (Table 2 , Entries 2-3). However, considering the importance of substituted thiophenes in pharmaceutical and materials 59 - 62 ,we focused our efforts on the more reactive 5-bromothiophene-2-carbaldehyde. We selected 2-substituted thiophenes over their 3-substituted counterparts because the halogen adjacent to the sulfur atom increases electrophilicity due to a stronger electron-withdrawing influence 63 . This adjustment allowed the reaction to proceed efficiently under mechanochemical conditions employing 7 mm stainless steel milling balls, yielding 51% of product 10 . The results indicate that the carbonyl group’s additional electron-withdrawing effect further enhances the ring’s electrophilicity, promoting the mechanochemical cross-coupling reaction.

The optimized mechanochemical SC conditions were next employed for the synthesis of three representative conjugated polymeric networks. This was achieved by the reaction of dihalogenated aryl substrates as π-conjugated linkers with TEB (Fig. 3 ).

These polymeric networks have unique extended π-conjugation and promising performance in practical applications of energy storage and photocatalysis. Their ability to facilitate efficient charge transport, coupled with their chemical stability, made them highly attractive for use in supercapacitors, batteries, and as photocatalysts for water splitting and pollutant degradation 4 , 6 , 28 , 64 . 1,4-Diiodobenzene ( BNZ ), 5,6-diiodobenzo[d][1,3]dioxole ( BDOX ), and 4,7-dibromobenzo[c][1,2,5]thiadiazole ( BDT ) were selected as neutral, electron-rich and electron-deficient aryl substrates, respectively, and coupled with TEB, which served as the core of the macromolecules. The three-directional growth pattern is conducive to forming a porous polymeric network. The dibrominated BDT was employed instead of a diiodo substrate, as brominated compounds are more widely available commercially. Due to BDT ’s high electron deficiency, it was expected to exhibit sufficient reactivity towards oxidative addition similar to 5-bromo-2-thiophenecarboxaldehyde (Fig. 2 , product 10). The multiple SC was successfully achieved through a solvent-free ball milling method employing the optimized conditions discussed above. The reaction proceeded for 60 min without temperature regulation or inert atmosphere control, aligning with green chemistry principles by minimising resource-intensive conditions. The formed macromolecules were washed with methanol and water several times and further purified by Soxhlet extraction. Solid-state ¹³C NMR spectra provided structural insights into all networks (Fig. 4 ), displaying the expected broad peaks typical of macromolecular/polymeric species associated with the TEB core species along with the characteristic resonance of acetylene carbons (-C ≡ C-) at 80-91 ppm 65 . Peaks appearing at 121-123 ppm are attributed to unprotonated aromatic carbons bonded to the acetylene bridges, while those between 128 and 133 ppm correspond to the unsubstituted aromatic carbons located near the -C ≡ C- bridges. BDOX and BDT displayed distinctive signals for aromatic C-N and C-O groups at 148 ppm and 153 ppm, respectively 66 ..

The FTIR spectra of the products in Fig. 5 further confirm the formation of the conjugated polymeric networks. The absorption band in the 3276-3333 cm [- 1 range corresponds to the terminal alkyne (≡ C-H) stretching vibration in the TEB starting material, which was completely absent in all three products ( BNZ , BDT , and BDOX ). This disappearance indicates the complete consumption of the terminal alkyne groups, confirming the progress of the coupling reaction and the formation of the desired networks. The spectra also reveal absorption bands around 3000 cm [- 1 , and in the 1420-1650 cm [- 1 range, corresponding to the stretching vibrations of aromatic C = C bonds and the C-H stretching vibrations of aromatic (Ar-H) groups, further confirming the presence of aromatic structures in the macromolecules. Lastly, the absorption bands in the 2100-2300 cm [- 1 region are indicative of the stretching vibrations of carbon-carbon triple bonds (-C ≡ C-) in the macromolecular products 67 .

These FTIR findings, supported by the solid-state 13 C NMR spectral data, collectively validate the successful multiple couplings and the production of the conjugated polymeric networks. The multiple SC strategy presents a sustainable, rapid, and highly efficient route for developing advanced macromolecular materials for energy storage, optoelectronics, and catalysis, offering a greener alternative to traditional solution-based methods.

The powder XRD patterns of BNZ -, BDT -, and BDOX -based polymers (Figure S26) show broad diffraction features without any sharp reflections, confirming their amorphous nature. Such patterns are consistent with the highly cross-linked, disordered framework structures 1 .

The surface morphology of the polymers was examined by scanning electron microscopy (SEM). All three materials, BNZ , BDOX , and BDT , exhibit the irregular, rough, and granular textures that are characteristic of conjugated porous polymers (Fig. 6 ). The BNZ sample shows agglomerated particulate domains with a sponge-like appearance, while BDOX and BDT display relatively compact surfaces containing uneven pores and interconnected aggregates. These disordered morphologies are consistent with the amorphous and cross-linked nature of CPP-type materials formed via step-growth coupling polymerizations.

The Brunauer-Emmett-Teller (BET) method was applied to determine the specific surface areas of the polymers over a relative pressure (P/P₀) range of 0.05-0.35 (Figures S27-29). The measured BET surface areas were 227.7 m² g⁻¹ for BNZ , 69.7 m² g⁻¹ for BDOX , and 40.1 m² g⁻¹ for BDT , values consistent with those typically observed for conjugated porous polymers. Conventional solution-polymerized analogues generally exhibit higher surface areas, ~ 270 m² g⁻¹ for BDT and 300-900 m² g⁻¹ for BNZ-based networks, depending on synthetic conditions 12 68 .,- 69 Although the current values are lower, they are encouraging for a first demonstration of a greener, mechanochemical synthesis route and indicate clear potential for further improvement. Optimization of the milling environment 70 and the use of silica 68 templating are expected to enhance surface area and tune pore structure in future work.

CONCLUSION
Herein, we report the optimization of mechanochemical, ball-milling-mediated triple Sonogashira coupling with a range of substrates suitable for the synthesis of functional molecules and conjugated polymeric networks. Importantly, our approach offers significant advantages, including the applicability to a broad range of substrates and the elimination of the need for additional heating or additives. Under optimized reaction conditions (CuI 5 mol%, PdCl 2 (PPh 3 ) 2 5 mol%, 11 eq. TEA, ball milling at 25 Hz and ambient aerobic conditions), the target model product, 1,3,5-tris(phenylethynyl)benzene ( TPEB ) was obtained in a yield as high as 92%. The use of aryl iodides with various key functional groups and electronic properties was evaluated under the above optimal conditions in their reaction with triethynylbenzene and, with the exception of amino-containing reagents, were shown to proceed smoothly, yielding novel tri-arylated conjugated aromatic species with acetylene linkers products in yields as high as 92%. The extension of the evaluation to halogenated thiophene derivatives highlighted the challenges associated with using less activated/electrophilic aryl substrates. Although the reaction of 5-bromothiophene-2-carbaldehyde with triethynylbenzene gave the target product in a 51% yield, suggesting that further investigations are warranted for these types of challenging substrates. The utility of this mechanochemical approach is demonstrated by the successful synthesis of three conjugated porous polymers. Our method significantly reduces reaction times and energy consumption while eliminating hazardous solvents, paving the way for sustainable production. Future efforts will focus on optimization of the milling parameters to achieve precise and predictable control over porosity.

METHODS
This procedure involved mixing triethynylbenzene (1 eq) with monohalogenated aromatic compounds (4.5 eq) in the presence of copper sources (5 mol%) and palladium sources (5 mol%), along with 11 eq. of a base. The mixture was placed in a 10 mL milling jar, along with five milling balls (Ø) of different sizes (4, 5, 7 mm), and was subjected to mechanical agitation using a ball mill. The reaction was carried out solvent-free at room temperature under normal air conditions, with the milling instrument set to operate at a speed of 25 Hz for 1 h. Upon completion of the reaction, the mixture was extracted with dichloromethane (DCM) to separate the organic phase. The organic layer was then treated with anhydrous sodium sulfate (Na 2 SO 4 ) to remove residual moisture. After drying, the solvent was evaporated under reduced pressure to obtain a concentrated residue. The crude product was further purified by column chromatography using silica gel as the stationary phase. A suitable elution solvent, such as a mixture of hexane and ethyl acetate or dichloromethane, was used to isolate the desired coupling products with high purity.

BNZ A stainless-steel milling jar (10 mL), equipped with 5 stainless-steel balls (7 mm Ø), was charged with fixed amounts of 1,3,5-triethynylbenzene (50.0 mg, 0.33 mmol, 1.0 eq), CuI (3.00 mg, 0.033 mmol, 0.1 eq), TEA (377.0 mg, 3.73 mmol, 11.0 eq), PdCl 2 (PPh 3 ) 2 (24.0 mg, 0.033 mmol, 0.1 eq), and 1,4-diiodobenzene (BNZ) (169.0 mg, 0.499 mmol, 1.5 eq). The reaction was carried out under solvent-free and normal air conditions via mechanical grinding. The jar was sealed and placed in the vibrational ball mill and subjected to milling for 5 sets of 90 min at 25 Hz. After that, the mixture was washed with methanol and water several times. Further purification was performed by Soxhlet extraction (20-25 mL of methanol as a solvent) for 24 h followed by drying at 80 °C for 24 h. Yield = 71%.

The same procedure was repeated for the synthesis of BDT and BDOX , and TEA or Cs 2 CO 3 can be used to achieve the products.

BDT : reagent: 4,7-dibromobenzo [c][1,2,5]thiadiazole (147.0 mg, 0.499 mmol, 1.5 eq), yield (66 mg, 89%).

BDOX : reagent: 5,6-Dibromo-1,3-benzodioxole (140.0 mg, 0.499 mmol, 1.5 eq), yield (53 mg, 83%).
