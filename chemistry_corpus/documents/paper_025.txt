ABSTRACT
Abstract A critical bottleneck in photocatalytic H 2 O 2 production lies not only in the severe non-radiative energy losses incurred from enhancing light absorption, but also in a fundamental proton-electron kinetic imbalance. Here we introduce a light-heat-proton coupling strategy that harnesses the dissipated photothermal energy to activate proton dissociation from carboxylic acids, thereby creating a productive driving force for redox catalysis. A hydrogen-bonded organic framework (HOF-FJU-200) incorporating mixed-valence Fe 2+ /Fe 3+ clusters is constructed via a metalloligand approach. The intervalence charge transfer transitions within these clusters generate strong photothermal conversion while extending light absorption to the NIR-II region ( ~ 2500 nm). The resulting thermal energy activates proton dissociation from unpaired carboxylic acid groups, synchronizing proton release with photoinduced electron transfer. This cooperative mechanism effectively channels non-radiative heat into catalytic function, achieving an H 2 O 2 production rate of 10657 μmol·g -1 ·h -1 without sacrificial agents. By directly coupling light, heat, and proton dynamics within a single framework, this work establishes a general paradigm for utilizing non-radiative energy to regulate proton-driven photocatalytic reactions. Subject terms: Energy, Photocatalysis, Photocatalysis, Photocatalysis

Photocatalytic H₂O₂ production is limited not only by severe non-radiative energy losses but also by a fundamental imbalance between proton and electron transfer kinetics. Here, the authors develop a light-heat-proton coupling strategy that exploits dissipated photothermal energy to promote proton dissociation from carboxylic acids, creating a driving force for efficient redox catalysis and H₂O₂ generation.

INTRODUCTION
Hydrogen-bonded organic frameworks (HOFs) have recently emerged as promising multifunctional materials, although their development still lags behind that of metal-organic frameworks (MOFs) and covalent-organic frameworks (COFs) 1 - 9 . The uniqueness of HOFs, particularly the easy regeneration through simple recrystallization, solution processability, intrinsic self-healing capability, and more preferential flexible-robust nature, has enabled scientists to develop a series of HOFs for their applications on gas storage, gas separation, molecular recognition, proton conduction, and sensing 2 , 10 - 13 . It is envisioned that HOFs can be developed as efficient photocatalysts as well, although this potential has been rarely fulfilled yet 14 .

Photocatalytic hydrogen peroxide (H 2 O 2 ) synthesis proceeds without sacrificial agents is an appealing route toward sustainable oxidant production, avoiding the energy-intensive anthraquinone process. To achieve efficient photocatalytic performance, materials must absorb a broad spectrum of light, particularly extending into the visible and near-infrared regions, to maximize the utilization of solar energy 15 . Traditional strategies to extend optical absorption, including band-gap narrowing in inorganic semiconductors and hybrid frameworks, often come at the cost of weakened redox potentials and heightened non-radiative recombination 16 . As a result, although heat can in some cases enhance reaction rates, a significant portion of the absorbed energy is lost through thermal dissipation pathways rather than being harnessed for chemical conversion.

Furthermore, the central challenge extends beyond light absorption to the precise control of the intricate pathways that govern energy utilization and dissipation. The photocatalytic synthesis of H 2 O 2 proceeds primarily via two half-reactions: the oxygen reduction reaction (ORR) and the water oxidation reaction (WOR). The desired 2-electron ORR pathway (O 2 + 2H⁺ + 2e⁻ → H 2 O 2 ) features intrinsically fast kinetics, which allows for the efficient utilization of photogenerated electrons. In contrast, the complementary WOR (via the 2-electron: 2H 2 O + 2 h⁺ → H 2 O 2 + 2H⁺ or 4-electron: 2H 2 O + 4 h⁺ → O 2 + 4H⁺) is intrinsically kinetically sluggish 17 . Therefore, a critical bottleneck arises from the common kinetic mismatch between proton generation and electron transfer, particularly under illumination (Fig. 1a ). The rate of proton generation often lags behind electron transfer kinetics, creating a local proton-deficient microenvironment at the catalytic sites 18 . This imbalance severely throttles the ORR pathway and limits the overall light-to-chemical efficiency.

HOFs featuring diverse hydrogen-bonding motifs and abundant π-π interactions, may provide an intrinsic platform to overcome this kinetic limitation. The dynamic and reversible nature of hydrogen bonds allows efficient proton dissociation and transfer, while π-π stacking among aromatic units promotes charge migration and suppresses charge recombination 19 - 22 . We hypothesize that in a specially designed HOF with strong near-infrared (NIR) absorption, the thermal energy generated from non-radiative relaxation can be harnessed to accelerate proton dissociation, thereby synchronizing proton and electron transfer during photocatalysis. Such a light-heat-proton coupled mechanism enables photogenerated carriers and thermally activated protons to act cooperatively, substantially boosting the photocatalytic efficiency for H 2 O 2 production (Fig. 1b ). While photothermal-enhanced H 2 O 2 production has been demonstrated in polymeric and single-atom catalytic systems, for example by Ye et al. and Wang et al. 23 , 24 , and NIR-responsive HOFs for photothermal conversion have been reported by Su et al. 25 , and proton-promoted H 2 O 2 synthesis has been previously described by Han et al. 26 , the “light-heat-proton coupling” mechanism has not been reported before and represents a novel approach to harnessing non-radiative energy for catalytic reactions. The realization of this concept, however, requires a material that simultaneously possesses efficient photothermal centers and readily accessible proton sources, a combination that remains elusive 27 .

Here we introduce a hydrogen-bonded organic framework, HOF-FJU-200, constructed through a metalloligand strategy involving mixed-valence Fe 2 ⁺/Fe 3 ⁺ clusters. The incorporation of these clusters endows the framework with exceptionally broad light absorption, extending from the visible to the near-infrared region, reaching up to 2500 nm through intervalence charge-transfer transitions. The π-π stacking interactions between adjacent ligands facilitate efficient intermolecular electron transfer, while the unpaired carboxyl groups act as proton sources that respond to the local photothermal effect. The combination of these features enables a synergistic interaction between photoexcitation and thermally induced proton dynamics. As a result, HOF-FJU-200 achieves an outstanding H 2 O 2 production rate of 10657 μmol·g -1 ·h -1 without the use of sacrificial agents, demonstrating an effective strategy for integrating photothermal and proton-coupled processes within a single soft framework material.

RESULTS
Dinuclear clathrochelate complexes bearing carboxylic acid functional groups (denoted as CLC-COOH) were successfully synthesized via metal-templated condensation reactions (Fig. 2a ). These reactions involved the combination of 4-cyanophenylboronic acid and 4-hydroxy-3,5-((hydroxyimide)methyl) benzoic acid, in the presence of both Fe 3+ and Fe 2+ salts. Subsequently, high-quality black octahedral single crystals of HOF-FJU-200 were obtained by slowly diffusing methanol into a solution of CLC-COOH in N,N-Dimethylformamide (DMF) (Supplementary Fig. 1a ). Notably, rapid pouring of methanol into the CLC-COOH-containing DMF solution yields uniformly sized HOF-FJU-200 nanoparticles (Supplementary Fig. 1b and Supplementary Fig. 1c ). Single-crystal X-ray diffraction (SCXRD) analysis confirms that the framework of HOF-FJU-200 assembled from CLC-COOH molecules as the fundamental building blocks (Fig. 2b ). X-ray photoelectron spectroscopy (XPS) indicates that the iron species in HOF-FJU-200 retain a mixed-valence state (Fe 2+ /Fe 3+ ) (Supplementary Fig. 2 ), which is also commonly observed in stable inorganic compounds such as Magnetite (Fe 3 O 4 ) and Prussian blue. Electrostatic Potential Surface (ESP) calculations reveal that cyano groups exhibit regions of pronounced negative electrostatic potential due to the electron-withdrawing effect, while carboxylic acid-substituted benzene displays positive potential areas (Fig. 2c ). This complementary charge distribution may significantly enhance the interactions between the two systems through π-π stacking. The weak hydrogen bonds formed between the oxygen atoms of boric acid and the CH groups on adjacent molecules further stabilize this assembly (Fig. 1d , Supplementary Fig. 3 ).

In fact, each of the four CLC-COOH molecules contributes only one benzonitrile group for the formation of the tetramer, leaving one remaining benzonitrile group on each molecule available to participate in the construction of adjacent tetramers (Fig. 2e ). Consequently, each triangular-pyramidal tetramer assembles with four neighboring triangular-pyramidal tetramers in the four corner-facing directions by sharing CLC-COOH molecules (Fig. 2e ). This connection pattern continues, resulting in the formation a dia topological structure (Fig. 2f ). Thus, HOF-FJU-200 can be regarded as π-interaction-driven hierarchical three-dimensional (3D) superstructures, which is quite challenging to achieve due to limited directional control and the weakness of intermolecular interactions 22 . Remarkably, unlike the conventional propensity of carboxylic-acid-based supramolecules to form hydrogen bonds, this unique stacking configuration does not depend on hydrogen-bonding interactions between carboxylic acids (Fig. 2 e, h ). This distinctive assembly mode endows HOF-FJU-200 with the rare characteristic of being a hydrogen-bonded organic framework featuring unpaired-carboxylate channels (Fig. 2 f, g ).

Unpaired-carboxylate channels have already been successfully engineered in MOF and COF families due to the directional assembly attributes 28 , 29 . Research has shown that carboxyl groups can facilitate proton conduction 30 , 31 . This function enables the conversion of superoxide (·O 2 - ), a key intermediate in H 2 O 2 photocatalysis, into H 2 O 2 . However, in HOFs, these functional groups often act as either strong hydrogen-bond donors or acceptors 32 , 33 . Consequently, they actively engage in intermolecular hydrogen bonding, contributing to framework formation and effectively immobilizing protons. This makes the preservation of unpaired carboxyl moieties exceedingly difficult in HOFs. Gao et al. reported a rare example of a HOF featuring unpaired carboxyl groups by utilizing paired residual carboxylic acids 34 . The assembly of a carboxylic acid-based HOF without employing any hydrogen bonding from the carboxylic acids, thereby keeping all carboxyl groups unpaired, had never been achieved prior to HOF-FJU-200.

Therefore, this distinctive property renders HOF-FJU-200 a promising candidate as a potential photocatalyst for H 2 O 2 production. Prior to photocatalyst study, esterified analogues of both the clathrochelate complex (CLC-COOCH 3 ) and its corresponding HOF framework (HOF-FJU-201) were synthesized (Supplementary Fig. 4a and Supplementary Fig. 4b ). SCXRD analysis reveals that in HOF-FJU-201, CLC-COOCH 3 molecules form dimers stabilized primarily by π-π stacking between cyanophenyl groups and methyl-ester-carboxylate phenyl rings (Supplementary Fig. 4c ). These dimers further assemble via C-H···O hydrogen bonding into 1D porous channels (Supplementary Fig. 4d - g ). Solid-state UV-Vis-NIR diffuse reflectance spectroscopy reveals dual-range light harvesting capabilities in these HOFs, demonstrating both strong visible absorption and pronounced NIR-II response (1000-2500 nm region, Fig. 3a ). This near-infrared absorption primarily originates from the mixed-valence Fe 2+ /Fe 3+ dinuclear sites that enable efficient NIR absorption via intervalence charge transfer (IVCT), a well-established phenomenon in Fe 2+ /Fe 3+ systems such as Magnetite (Fe 3 O 4 ) and Prussian blue analogs 35 . Tauc plot analyses reveal optical band gaps of 1.54 eV (HOF-FJU-200) and 1.43 eV (HOF-FJU-201) (Supplementary Fig. 5 ), which are substantially narrower than those of conventional HOFs ( > 2.0 eV). This narrowing enables HOF-COOR systems (R = H, CH 3 ) to efficiently harness low-energy solar photons, significantly advancing their potential for photon-functional applications.

Mott-Schottky measurements confirm n-type semiconductivity of the materials (positive slopes, Supplementary Fig. 6 ), with flat-band potentials at -0.78 V (HOF-FJU-200) and -0.65 V vs. Ag/AgCl (HOF-FJU-201). After conversion to the normal hydrogen electrode (NHE) using the factor +0.198 V, these values become -0.58 V for HOF-FJU-200 and -0.45 V vs. NHE for HOF-FJU-201. For n-type semiconductors, the conduction band is typically at least 0.10 eV more negative than the flat-band potential. Consequently, the conduction band (CB) positions were calculated to be -0.68 V for HOF-FJU-200 and -0.55 V vs. NHE for HOF-FJU-201. The favorable CB positions of HOF-FJU-200 and HOF-FJU-201 indicate their potential for H 2 O 2 production via the O 2 reduction reaction (ORR) (Fig. 3b ). The corresponding valence band (VB) positions at pH 7 (vs. NHE) are +0.86 V for HOF-FJU-200 and +0.88 V for HOF-FJU-201 (Fig. 3b ). Electrochemical impedance spectroscopy (EIS) revealed a smaller semicircular Nyquist radius for HOF-FJU-200 compared to HOF-FJU-201 (Fig. 3c ), indicating lower charge transfer resistance in HOF-FJU-200. Under photostimulation, HOF-FJU-200 exhibited a stronger transient photocurrent response than HOF-FJU-201, signifying superior efficiency in electron-hole separation and charge transfer within the HOF-FJU-200 framework (Fig. 3d ). These photoelectrochemical differences likely account for the significant disparity in photocatalytic H 2 O 2 production performance.

HOF-FJU-200 is stable in methanol, ethanol, isopropanol, n-propanol, toluene, diethyl ether, and even boiling water (Supplementary Figs. 7 - 11 ). It was treated with pure water for 10 exchange cycles to completely remove the guest solvent molecules within its pores (Supplementary Fig. 12 ), thereby eliminating their interference with the photocatalytic reaction. To evaluate their photocatalytic H 2 O 2 production capabilities, reactions were conducted under visible light irradiation (λ > 420 nm; 300 W Xe lamp) in O 2 -saturated water contained in 30 mL vials, without addition of any sacrificial agents. The concentration of generated H 2 O 2 was quantified by titration with Ce(SO 4 ) 2 . Strikingly, HOF-FJU-200 showcased a distinctive catalytic behavior that deviated from the established patterns commonly seen in prior photocatalytic materials. In most cases, the catalytic yield of these traditional materials usually follows either a linear progression with time or a gradual decrease. In contrast, during the initial half-hour, HOF-FJU-200 exhibited a relatively modest catalytic activity, registering a production rate of 4706 μmol·g -1 ·h -1 . However, beyond this phase, its activity abruptly surged to 10657 μmol·g -1 ·h -1 , transitioning into a sustained linear production regime (Fig. 4a ). HOF-FJU-200 cannot directly utilize low-energy infrared photons (λ > 810 nm) to drive H 2 O 2 production due to the band-gap limitation, whereas the 720-810 nm NIR light is effective (Supplementary Fig. 13 ). The apparent quantum yield (AQY) of HOF-FJU-200 was 11.58% at 420 nm, and at near-infrared wavelengths it reached 2.56% at 720 nm and 2.35% at 810 nm, demonstrating a broad spectral response and efficient use of NIR light for photocatalysis (Table S2 ). The solar-chemical conversion efficiency (SCC) of HOF-FJU-200 reached 0.162%. Furthermore, such self-accelerating performance exceeds all previously reported results for discrete molecule-based photocatalysts, including HOFs (Fig. 4c , Supplementary Tables 3 - 4 ). More significantly, it outperforms the best MOFs and rivals the top-performing COFs (Supplementary Tables 5 - 6 ). Single crystals of HOF-FJU-200 can be retained in an aqueous H 2 O 2 solution at a concentration of 0.1 M, far exceeding the concentration generated during photocatalysis (0.0005 M), which rules out H 2 O 2 -induced structural degradation (See CIF file: HOF-FJU-200-H 2 O 2 , Supplementary Fig. 14 ). Post-reaction PXRD, FT-IR, SCXRD and XPS analysis confirmed unchanged crystallinity (Supplementary Figs. 15 - 19 ) and five consecutive photocatalytic cycles demonstrated >95% retention of activity with simple catalyst recovery, collectively attesting to the material’s robust operational stability (Supplementary Fig. 20 ). Turn-on/turn-off cycling experiments confirm that HOF-FJU-200 exhibits excellent anti-photobleaching property with no significant loss of activity over repeated cycles (Supplementary Fig. 21 ).

The production rate of HOF-FJU-200 (10657 μmol·g -1 ·h -1 ) vastly surpassed that of HOF-FJU-201 (718 μmol·g -1 ·h -1 ) (Fig. 4 a, b ). To decouple packing structural contributions, we assessed their monomeric molecules, CLC-COOH and CLC-COOCH 3 . Powder X‑ray diffraction (PXRD) of the monomers shows that they are almost entirely amorphous at room temperature, indicating no long‑range periodic stacking (Supplementary Fig. 22 ). The CLC-COOH monomer exhibits substantially higher H 2 O 2 production than the CLC-COOCH 3 monomer (3951 vs 562 μmol·g -1 ·h -1 , respectively) (Fig. 4b ). Notably, both HOFs outperform their monomers (HOF-FJU-200: 10657 vs 3951; HOF-FJU-201: 718 vs 562 μmol·g -1 ·h -1 ), demonstrating that it is the accessible pore channels within the HOF frameworks, rather than only the external surface molecules, that facilitate mass transport and contribute significantly to the enhanced catalytic generation of H 2 O 2 (Fig. 4b ).

To elucidate the origin of this activity surge, we systematically evaluated HOF-FJU-200’s photocatalytic performance under controlled thermal conditions. In the absence of light, H 2 O 2 production was negligible, demonstrating that light, rather than heat, is the indispensable component for catalysis (Fig. 4d ). Under illumination, the activity exhibited Arrhenius-type enhancement with increasing temperature ( < 80 °C), confirming thermal acceleration of the catalytic kinetics (Fig. 4d ). Significantly, the measured activity at 80 °C ( ~ 10000 μmol·g -1 ·h -1 ) precisely matched the post-surge rate observed in the kinetic profile. Under isothermal conditions at 20 °C to minimize the thermal effects, HOF-FJU-200 (with free -COOH) yielded 2406 μmol·g -1 ·h -1 , whereas the esterified analogue HOF-FJU-201 (with -COOCH 3 ) yielded only 370 μmol·g -1 ·h -1 . This trend persisted in the amorphous monomer controls: CLC-COOH gave 1385 μmol·g -1 ·h -1 while CLC-COOCH 3 gave 255 μmol·g -1 ·h -1 . The consistent superiority of the carboxylic acid-containing species across both crystalline and amorphous states confirms that internal proton availability is critical for enhanced catalysis, independent of morphology or packing effects (Supplementary Fig. 23 ). Beyond this threshold, activity declined markedly due to thermally-driven H 2 O 2 decomposition (2H 2 O 2 → 2H 2 O + O 2 ). Subsequent H 2 O 2 decomposition tests confirmed this deduction: almost no decomposition occurred at 80 °C (Supplementary Fig. 24 - 25 ), while significant decomposition was observed at 100 °C (Supplementary Fig. 26 ). This temperature-dependent behavior, particularly the congruence at 80 °C, establishes that the abrupt activity surge originates from in-situ thermal elevation within the catalytic system during photocatalysis.

Under 808-nm laser irradiation (0.5 W/cm 2 ), solid HOF-FJU-200 crystals rapidly heated to 166 °C within 10 seconds (heating rate >12 °C/s) (Fig. 4 f- h ), while the corresponding aqueous photocatalytic system reached 78 °C in 30 min (Fig. 4 e- g ). HOF-FJU-201 exhibits comparable photothermal conversion efficiency with HOF-FJU-200 (Supplementary Fig. 27 ). Fluorescence spectroscopy revealed negligible emission intensity from HOF-FJU-200 and HOF-FJU-201, indicating severe non-radiative decay under illumination (Supplementary Fig. 28 ). This complete quenching of emission is attributed to an intervalence charge transfer (IVCT) effect, which provides an efficient non-radiative decay pathway that suppresses radiative recombination 36 . Consequently, the mixed-valence Fe 2+ /Fe 3+ dinuclear sites in HOF-FJU-200 and HOF-FJU-201 enable ultra-efficient photothermal conversion through synergistic mechanisms: intervalence charge transfer (IVCT) broadens near-infrared (NIR) absorption, and polaron formation accelerates thermal relaxation 37 . This synergistic interplay indicates a novel light-heat-proton coupling pathway in HOF-FJU-200, where light absorption, heat generation, and carboxylate-facilitated proton transport collectively drive the enhanced photocatalytic H 2 O 2 production. In contrast, HOF-FJU-201 is constructed from ester-based monomers. Although it exhibits comparable photothermal efficiency, it lacks the ability to dissociate protons internally. Without an intrinsic proton source, the reaction must rely on inert water molecules to supply protons, a kinetically sluggish process that renders its self-accelerating catalytic behavior far less pronounced than that of HOF-FJU-200.

To further elucidate the mechanisms of H 2 O 2 generation by HOF-FJU-200, a series of experiments was conducted to identify the key species. The proton conduction behaviors of HOF-FJU-200 and HOF-FJU-201 were analyzed using alternating current (AC) impedance spectroscopy on pelletized samples under controlled humidity and temperature (Supplementary Fig. 29 ). At 90% relative humidity (RH), the proton conductivity of both materials exhibited a significant increase with rising temperature (Fig. 5a , Supplementary Table 8 ). Neglecting the thermal decomposition of H 2 O 2 , these dissociated protons facilitate the photocatalytic reaction with superoxide radicals (·O 2 - ) to produce H 2 O 2 (·O 2 - + 2H + +e - →H 2 O 2 ). Notably, the proton conductivity of HOF-FJU-200 was significantly higher than that of HOF-FJU-201, with corresponding activation energy ( E ₐ) values of 0.304 eV and 0.405 eV, respectively (Figs. 5 a, b and Supplementary Table 8 ). This indicates that the presence of carboxylic acid groups facilitates proton dissociation, thereby promoting H 2 O 2 synthesis, which is consistent with the substantially higher catalytic activity observed for HOF-FJU-200 compared to HOF-FJU-201.

H 2 O 2 formation was negligible in the dark and significantly reduced under light illumination in a N 2 atmosphere (Fig. 5c ), demonstrating that both O 2 and light are essential for photocatalytic H 2 O 2 production. In the presence of sodium iodate (NaIO 3 ) and lanthanum oxide (La 2 O 3 ), HOF-FJU-200 can photocatalyse H 2 O to O 2 confirmed by online gas chromatography (GC) analysis (Supplementary Table 7 ). Scavenger studies with AgNO 3 (e - acceptor), tert -butanol (TBA, ∙OH quencher), and p -benzoquinone (pBQ, ·O 2 - scavenger) demonstrated: TBA induced no significant activity suppression, ruling out ∙OH involvement; pBQ reduced H 2 O 2 production to near-background levels, confirming ·O 2 - as indispensable; AgNO 3 caused substantial activity loss, verifying photogenerated electron participation (Fig. 5c ). Parallel control experiments on HOF-FJU-201 showed similar mechanistic dependencies. EPR spectroscopy with 5,5-dimethyl-1-pyrroline N-oxide (DMPO) spin-trapping revealed no detectable ·O 2 - signals in darkness but distinct DMPO-·O 2 - adducts upon illumination, with HOF-FJU-200 exhibiting greater signal intensity than HOF-FJU-201, which correlates positively with its superior H 2 O 2 performance (Fig. 5d ). This integrated evidence establishes a sequential two-electron oxygen reduction reaction (ORR) pathway: O 2 + e - →·O 2 - followed by ·O 2 - + 2H + +e - →H 2 O 2 , where photogenerated electrons and superoxide radicals constitute fundamental active species.

In situ Fourier Transform Infrared (FTIR) spectroscopy was employed to dynamically track intermediates formed during photocatalytic H 2 O 2 generation over HOF-FJU-200 and HOF-FJU-201. The spectra were collected at 10-minute intervals under Xe lamp irradiation with continuous O 2 flow, using samples pre-wetted with water. Progressive spectral evolution revealed three key features: the O-O stretching vibration at ~945 cm -1 intensified with irradiation time (Fig. 5 e, f ), while emerging peaks at ~1177 cm -1 (assigned to ·O 2 - ) and ~1237 cm -1 (OOH*) confirmed superoxide radical and hydroperoxyl intermediate formation. In addition, The vibrational band observed in the 1000-1030 cm -1 range, assigned to the C-O-O stretching mode, confirms the adsorption of O 2 on aromatic carbon sites 38 . The progressive enhancement of the peroxide (·O 2 - ) intermediate-derived C-O ( ~ 1280-1290 cm -1 ) and O-O ( ~ 1050-1060 cm -1 ) stretching bands, is accompanied by a cumulative enhancement of H 2 O 2 -associated O-O bending vibration ( ~ 1320-1340 cm -1 ) 39 . Furthermore, the vibrational feature observed in the 1095-1105 cm -1 region are ascribed to intermediates of water oxidation 40 . Notably, HOF-FJU-200 exhibits a more pronounced intensity of ·O 2 - signal than HOF-FJU-201. This enhancement can be attributed to the deprotonated carboxylic acid groups of HOF-FJU-200, which promotes efficient and sustained electron transfer to O 2 , thereby favoring the continuous formation and accumulation of ·O 2 - species.

Significantly, new vibrational peaks associated with -COO - groups appeared near 1430 cm -1 in HOF-FJU-200 under illumination (Fig. 5f ). This indicates that a fraction of the carboxyl groups in HOF-FJU-200 underwent proton dissociation during the photocatalytic reaction, undergoing reversible interconversion between -COOH and -COO - , which aligns with our earlier hypothesis. This phenomenon was not observed for HOF-FJU-201. Collectively, these time-resolved vibrational signatures unambiguously corroborate the proposed two-electron ORR pathway (O 2 → ·O 2 - → OOH* → H 2 O 2 ). Rotating ring-disk electrode (RRDE) experiments were performed to investigate the selectivity toward the oxygen reduction reaction (ORR) pathway. Linear sweep voltammetry (LSV) curves of two HOFs were recorded in an O 2 -saturated 0.1 M phosphate buffer solution at different rotating speeds (Supplementary Fig. 30a, b ). The calculated n value for HOF-FJU-200 was 2.01, which is closer to 2 than that of HOF-FJU-201 (2.34), indicating that HOF-FJU-200 exhibits higher O 2 -to-H 2 O 2 selectivity under the same conditions (Supplementary Fig. 30 c ).

Density functional theory (DFT) calculations comparing the reaction pathways of HOF-FJU-200 and HOF-FJU-201 revealed critical mechanistic insights. While HOF-FJU-200 frameworks feature potential catalytic sites (-COOH, -CN, -CH groups), the carboxylic acid moiety demonstrates energetically favorable catalysis compared to alternative sites (Fig. 6a and Supplementary Fig. 31 ), establishing -COOH as the dominant active center. In HOF-FJU-201, the carbonyl group also represents the most suitable catalytic site (Fig. 6a and Supplementary Fig. 32 ). It is well established that the *OOH intermediate is a crucial step in H 2 O 2 synthesis. Crucially, during the conversion from *O 2 to *OOH, HOF-FJU-200 exhibits a significantly lower energy barrier compared to HOF-FJU-201 (Fig. 6b and Supplementary Figs. 3 and 34 ). Additional calculations demonstrate that the free carboxyl groups within the framework, once deprotonated, can significantly lower the energy barriers (Δ G = 0.07420 eV) of the critical reaction steps compared to their non-deprotonated counterparts (Δ G = 0.31250 eV). Although the energy barrier of the deprotonated HOF-FJU-200 increases slightly with temperature (Supplementary Fig. 35 , Supplementary Table 9 ), the values remain much smaller than those of the non-deprotonated system. This confirms that the decisive factor underlying the superior performance of HOF-FJU-200 is the temperature-promoted deprotonation of carboxylic acid groups, which supplies protons on demand to the catalytic sites. Therefore, based on experimental and computational results, a possible mechanism is proposed (Fig. 6c ): Initially, O 2 is adsorbed onto the -COOH sites. Upon light irradiation, the temperature of the HOF framework increases, promoting proton generation through dissociation of carboxylic acid groups and H 2 O. Simultaneously, the organic segment generates photogenerated electrons, and then adsorbed O 2 obtains the electron and proton to generate *OOH intermediate. Subsequently, *OOH acquires a second proton and electron to produce H 2 O 2 . This proton transfer process can also facilitate water dissociation, boosting O 2 evolution.

In summary, this work establishes a light-heat-proton coupling strategy within a hydrogen-bonded organic framework to address the challenges in photocatalytic hydrogen peroxide (H 2 O 2 ) synthesis. By incorporating mixed-valence Fe 2+ /Fe 3+ clusters, this framework achieves broad optical absorption extending into the NIR-II region ( ~ 2500 nm) via intervalence charge transfer. This enhancement enables exceptional photothermal conversion efficiency, driving proton dissociation from carboxylic acid groups and promoting proton-coupled electron transfer during photocatalysis. This cooperative coupling enables a highly efficient and self-accelerating photocatalytic process, delivering a record H 2 O 2 production rate of 10657 μmol·g -1 ·h -1 without sacrificial agents. By directly coupling light absorption, heat generation, and proton dynamics within a hydrogen-bonded organic framework, this study introduces a new paradigm that transforms non-radiative thermal loss into catalytic functionality, thereby offering a general blueprint for a wide range of proton-coupled electron transfer reactions beyond H 2 O 2 synthesis.

METHODS
Black polyhedral crystals of HOF-FJU-200 were obtained by dissolving 5 mg of CLC-COOH in 1 mL of DMF via sonication; the solution was then placed in a 20 mL vial containing 3 mL of methanol for a 5-day diffusion, after which the crystals were collected by filtration and washed with methanol.

Black strip crystals of HOF-FJU-201 were obtained by dissolving 5 mg of CLC-COOCH 3 in 1 mL of DMA via sonication; the solution was then placed in a 20 mL vial containing 3 mL of methanol for a 5-day diffusion, after which the crystals were collected by filtration and washed with methanol.

Black nanoparticles of HOF-FJU-200 were synthesized by first dissolving 50 mg of CLC-COOH in 10 mL of DMF with sonication. Subsequently, 50 mL of methanol was added immediately, and the mixture was stirred for 2 h. After the resulting suspension was allowed to stand for 24 h, the black nanoparticles were collected via centrifugation (three cycles) and washed with methanol.

Black nanoparticles of HOF-FJU-201 were synthesized by first dissolving 50 mg of CLC-COOCH 3 in 10 mL of DMA with sonication. Subsequently, 50 mL of methanol was added immediately, and the mixture was stirred for 2 hours. After the resulting suspension was allowed to stand for 24 h, the black nanoparticles were collected via centrifugation (three cycles) and washed with methanol.
