ABSTRACT
Visible light photocatalysis has reshaped synthetic organic
chemistry
by enabling mild and selective radical processes. However, its widespread
adoption is still constrained by the reliance on homogeneous photocatalysts,
typically precious-metal complexes or organic dyes, which complicate
recovery, recyclability, scalability, and integration into telescoped
continuous flow processes. Heterogeneous photocatalysts (hPCs) offer
a sustainable alternative, and among emerging candidates, π-conjugated
polymers (π-CPs) have recently attracted attention as tunable,
metal-free semiconductors capable of efficient visible light harvesting.
This perspective highlights poly­(arylene-ethynylene)­s (PAEs), a distinctive
subclass of π-CPs whose modular, conjugated backbones enable
control over morphology, band structure, and excited-state behavior.
Historically developed for optoelectronic applications, PAEs are now
gaining traction as photocatalysts in synthetic organic chemistry,
including in continuous flow reactors. Yet, the absence of systematic
structure-reactivity relationships limits rational catalyst
design and restricts broader deployment. Here, we examine emerging
trends that link PAEs structure to photocatalytic function and articulate
guiding principles for designing next-generation polymeric photocatalysts.
By integrating concepts from materials science and synthetic methodology,
this perspective aims to establish PAEs as robust, recyclable, and
versatile platforms for sustainable photocatalysis.

INTRODUCTION
Over the past few decades,
visible light photocatalysis has become
a cornerstone of modern organic synthesis, enabling mild and selective
processes that unlock radical reactivity, challenging bond constructions,
and previously inaccessible reaction pathways. While homogeneous molecular photocatalysts (PCs) have driven many
of these advances, their broader implementation
is constrained by limited catalyst recovery, photostability, and compatibility
with sustainable continuous or multistep processing. These limitations have spurred increasing interest in heterogeneous
organic semiconductor photocatalysts (hPCs), which combine molecular-level tunability with the inherent recyclability
of solid-state materials, including straightforward integration in
continuous flow systems.

Among emerging
hPC platforms, conjugated polymers (π-CPs)
have attracted attention due to their modular synthesis, broad visible
light absorption, and structural robustness under photocatalytic conditions. Within this class, poly­(arylene-ethynylene)­s
(PAEs)constructed from aromatic units linked by ethynylene
spacersoccupy a distinctive position. PAEs are readily accessible from commercially available building
blocks via well-established cross-coupling strategies, enabling their
tuning for a wide range of photocatalytic applications. Although their
semiconducting properties have been recognized for more than two decades,
PAEs have historically been applied in molecular electronics and photonic
materials. Only recently, driven by the
resurgence of visible light photocatalysis, PAEs have emerged as heterogeneous
photocatalysts for organic transformations. Key to their reactivity
is the efficient π-electron delocalization upon irradiation,
which generates long-lived excited states capable of productive single
electron and energy transfer events (SET and EnT, respectively, Scheme ). ,

Despite this promise, the development of PAE-based
photocatalysts
has largely proceeded through empirical exploration with limited articulation
of general structure-reactivity relationships. This has hindered
rational catalyst design and constrained their broader adoption in
synthetic organic chemistry. We contend, however, that the accessibility
and structural regularity of PAEs make them particularly well suited
as model systems for probing structure-reactivity relationships
in heterogeneous photocatalysis.

In parallel, while continuous
flow photocatalysis has been widely
recognized as a strategy to overcome photon- and mass-transfer limitations
inherent to batch photochemical reactions, the use of PAEs in flow systems remains comparatively underdeveloped.

In this perspective, we analyze recent advances in PAE-based heterogeneous
visible light photocatalysis with an emphasis on identifying unifying
structure-reactivity principles and highlighting opportunities
enabled by continuous flow operation. Our goal is to establish a conceptual
and forward-looking framework for the rational design and implementation
of PAEsand organic semiconductor photocatalysts more broadlyin
synthetic photocatalysis, spanning material design, reaction development,
and flow-based processing. Finally, we discuss future directions of
research and identify key advances needed to fully exploit the potential
of PAEs as heterogeneous photocatalysts in synthetic organic chemistry.

BACKGROUND ON PAES PHOTOCATALYSIS
To
illustrate the defining structural and photophysical characteristics
of PAEs, poly­( para -phenylene ethynylene) ( 1 )the simplest member of this family, composed exclusively
of benzene ringsis considered as a model system ( Scheme a). In its ground-state, 1 is a rigid, rod-like polymer that adopts a conformation
of successively twisted phenylacetylene repeat units. It is insoluble in common organic solvents and displays
strong visible light absorption, with an absorption tail extending
beyond 800 nm. Despite high thermal stability (>300 °C), polymer 1 exhibits a very low rotational barrier (<1 kcal·mol -1 ), enabling facile backbone
planarization under mild activation. Accordingly, visible light irradiation
promotes rotation of π-orbitals between adjacent units, generating
a planarized, conjugated backbone and triggering π-electron
delocalization over approximately 3-10 repeat units. The resulting excited-state ( 1* ) is commonly described as a cumulene-like (or quinoid ) structure displaying separated positive and negative charges, where
reactions can take place. As we will see later, the charge separation
and migration strongly influence the excited-state lifetimes of PAEs
and therefore their reactivity. As for the simplest PAE 1 , the triplet excited-state lifetime ( τ) reaches ca. 200 μs, sufficiently
long to sustain productive photocatalytic processes.

In general, the excited-state electronic structure
of PAEs is commonly
described using simplified energy diagrams based on the semiconductor
band-theory formalism ( Scheme a, lower left). In the ground-state
(i.e., the twisted backbone), electrons occupy the valence band (VB);
upon photoexcitation with energy exceeding the band gap ( E G ), electrons are promoted to the conduction band (CB),
generating excitons (or electron-hole pairs, i.e., excited-states).
As noted above, the extended conjugation of PAEs facilitates π-electron
delocalization, enabling spatial separation and migration of the electron-hole
pair (i.e., the quinoid form). Because
the charge transport is dominated by photoexcited electrons that move
toward the material surface, PAEs are classified as n-type semiconductors.

Photoexcited
PAEs accumulate electrons at their surface and are intrinsically predisposed
toward single-electron reduction chemistry. This electronic bias rationalizes the widespread use of molecular
oxygen as a reaction partner in PAE-based heterogeneous photocatalysis,
where O 2 functions as the excited-electron quencher. As
a result, aerobic oxidations, including the conversion of sulfides
to sulfoxides, amines to imines, and arylboronic acids to phenols,
constitute the most extensively reported transformations ( Scheme b). These reactions
may proceed through complementary mechanistic pathways, encompassing
SET processes that generate superoxide (O 2 •- ) and EnT pathways involving singlet oxygen ( 1 O 2 ). The catalytic cycle depicted in Scheme b for amine oxidation
is likewise applicable to sulfide and arylboronic acid oxidations,
although the latter requires a sacrificial tertiary amine (R 3 N).

STRUCTURE–REACTIVITY RELATIONSHIP OF
PAES
Understanding
how structure governs photocatalytic activity is
essential for moving beyond the empirical optimization of PAEs toward
rational catalyst design. In these materials, subtle variations at
both the molecular level and the macromolecular level can profoundly
influence light absorption, charge separation, and interfacial reactivity.
Drawing on selected literature, the following section examines how
these structural parameters collectively modulate the photophysical
and catalytic behavior of solid-state PAEs, with the goal of establishing
clear structure-activity relationships. Specifically, we address the
following questions: What role does the CC linkage play in
governing the reactivity of the PAEs? How do substituents on the aromatic
rings modulate the PAE reactivity? How do macromolecular characteristicssuch
as surface area, porosity, and particle sizeimpact the overall
photocatalytic activity of PAEs ( Figure )?

Structure-reactivity relationship study
of PAEs at the molecular
and macromolecular scales.

The CC triple bond is an inherent structural feature of
PAEs, but its specific role in determining their reactivity is crucial.
A study led by Lan and Bai addressed this aspect by comparing the
photocatalytic activity of triazine-derived PAEs incorporating different
linkers: single (C-C, 2 ), double (CC, 3 ), and triple bonds (CC, 4 ), in benchmark
oxidation reactions of thioanisole 5 and benzylamine 7 . Across all cases, CC
triple bond-containing PAE 4 consistently afforded the
highest yields of the oxidized products99% of sulfoxide 6 and imine 8 when used as a hPC. Comprehensive
analytical studies were performed on materials 2 - 4 , revealing distinct differences in their optical properties.
In the absorption spectra, PAE 4 exhibited a markedly
enhanced absorption profile in the visible region ( abs λ tail ( 4 ) > 800 nm) relative to 3 ( abs λ tail ( 3 ) = 750 nm)
and 2 ( abs λ tail ( 2 ) =
680 nm). These spectral differences correlated with the band gaps,
which decreased progressively from 2.05 eV for 2 to 1.92
eV for 3 , and from 1.86 eV for 4 . The measurement
of redox potentials demonstrated that all materials could reduce dioxygen
( E red [O 2 /O 2 •- ] = -0.59 V vs SCE) via SET at the CB (excited electrons), with hPC 4 displaying the highest reductive power ( E [ 4e- ] = -1.30 V vs SCE). However, the redox potentials
at the VB (holes) were thermodynamically insufficient to promote single
electron oxidations on thioanisole ( E ox [PhSMe/PhS •+ Me] = +1.59 V vs SCE), discrediting the photoredox pathway proposed by the authors.
Instead, we concluded that an EnT pathway via 1 O 2 might be the main mechanism. Finally, photoluminescence and time-resolved
fluorescence spectroscopy revealed that 4 possessed the
longest singlet excited-state lifetime (34 ns), slightly exceeding
those of 3 (30 ns) and 2 (23 ns). Although
these lifetimes cannot be directly correlated with the predominant
EnT pathway, they nonetheless reflect enhanced exciton stability in 4 , accounting for its better catalytic activity ( Scheme ).

Taken together, these results demonstrate that
incorporation of
π-linkers into the polymer backbone markedly enhances the photocatalytic
efficiency in oxidative transformations. This improvement stems from
the increased conjugation, as revealed by spectroscopic analyses,
which broadens absorption into the visible region and enables more
efficient light harvesting. Although the differences between CC
and CC π-linkers are relatively subtle here, the absorption
profiles and band gap measurements indicate that CC linkers
provide improved absorption characteristics thanks to their linear
geometry, which reduces steric hindrance, strengthens the orbital
overlap, and enhances conjugation. This results in more effective
electron delocalization upon irradiation and prolonged excited-state
lifetimes, ultimately giving better photocatalytic efficiency. The
CC motif also increases the single electron reducing power
of the polymers, as reflected in their redox potentials. Collectively,
these attributes confer improved catalytic performance to PAEs, consistent
with additional studies (not shown here) that underscored the pivotal
role of alkyne linkers in conjugated polymers (Practical Highlight
1).

High conjugation is a key driver of the light harvesting capabilities
of PAEs. A critical design element in conjugated PAE systems is the
positioning of the CC triple bonds on the aromatic core. While
most reported PAEs incorporate meta -trisubstituted
aromatic linkers (e.g., 1,3,5-triethynylbenzene), resulting in nonfully
conjugated backbones, our group has demonstrated that fully conjugated
linkers can significantly enhance photocatalytic performances ( Scheme ).

To evaluate this effect, three poly­(phenylene-ethynylene)­s
(PPEs)
were synthesized: the fully conjugated linear PPE ( 1 ),
the 1,3-branched ( 9 ), and the 1,3,5-trisubstituted one
( 10 ). In the model benzylamine oxidation reaction, the
expected imine product 8 was obtained in comparable yields
using 1 and 9 (67% and 66%, respectively)
and in slightly lower yield with 10 (60%). Notably, under
continuous flow conditions, photocatalyst 1 delivered
a quantitative yield (100%), whereas 9 and 10 afforded only 72%. Photophysical studies revealed a lower band gap
for 1 (2.60 eV) and 9 (2.54 eV) relative
to 10 (2.79 eV), consistent with their absorption profiles.
Interestingly, material 10 exhibited a higher oxidative
power ( E [ 10h + ] = +1.86 V vs SCE) compared with 9 ( E [ 9h + ] = +1.76 V vs SCE) and 1 ( E [ 1h + ] = +1.67 V vs SCE). Also, PAEs 1 and 10 displayed a higher reductive power (-0.93 V vs SCE) in comparison
with 9 ( E [ 9e - ] = -0.78 V vs SCE). Additionally, the
changes in aromatic substitutions afforded very different surface
areas for the three materials (calculated with the Brunauer-Emmett-Teller
method, BET). As such, 1,4- and 1,3-disubstituted materials 1 and 9 displayed no porosity ( S BET < 50 m 2 g -1 ), while
1,3,5-trisubstituted polymer 10 was highly porous ( S BET ( 10 ) = 1031 m 2 g -1 ). Mechanistic investigations indicated that both
photoredox and energy transfer pathways were operative under these
conditions.

Therefore, these investigations demonstrated that
linear 1,4-diethynylbenzene
precursors are better suited for heterogeneous photocatalysis in general,
thanks to the better conjugation of the polymer. It also highlighted
that modifying the position of the CC triple bond on the aromatic
rings had a non-negligible influence on the redox potentials and that
higher redox potentials and higher porosity do not necessarily mean
higher reactivity. Additionally, the differences in reactivity between
batch and flow systems revealed that traditional batch conditions
can be sometimes limited for distinguishing subtle differences in
photocatalysts’ performance. In contrast, flow systems, due
to their enhanced surface-area-to-volume ratio and more homogeneous
irradiation, enable accelerated reaction kinetics and more accurate
comparison of catalyst efficiencies. In this case, the reaction time
decreased from 19 h in batch to 9 min in flow, allowing for clear
differentiation among the three photocatalysts, which appeared nearly
equivalent under batch conditions (Practical Highlight 2).

Conjugated
polymers require planar architectures to enable efficient charge transport.
Such planarity may arise either from rotational relaxation upon excitation
or from an intrinsically planar ground-state structure. A study conducted
by Zhang, Ruan, Qian, and Rueping highlighted this effect by incorporating
various 9-fluorenone derivatives with different branching patterns
into pyrene-derived PAEs (C2-branched 11 ; C3-branched 12 , Scheme a). Interestingly, branching patterns
strongly influenced the ground-state planarity of the polymers. DFT
calculations showed that polymer 11 was fully planar,
whereas polymer 12 adopted a curved geometry. Also, 11 displayed a slightly lower band gap (1.78 eV) than 12 (1.87 eV), rationalized from minor differences in their
absorption profiles. Submitting 11 and 12 in the SET-mediated photocatalytic aerobic oxidation of N -aryl tetrahydroisoquinolines (such as 13 )
demonstrated the superior catalytic activity of 11 , which
afforded a 96% yield of product 14 (instead of 26% for 12 ). In addition to its improved planarity, the measurement
of redox potentials also provided insights into the better reactivity
of 11 . While hole redox potential measurements (1.05
and 1.30 V vs SCE for 11 and 12 , respectively)
demonstrated that both materials could thermodynamically oxidize N -aryl tetrahydroisoquinolines ( E ox = +0.89 V vs SCE), the CB potentials
(-0.73 V vs SCE for 11 and -0.57 V vs SCE
for 12 ) highlighted that 11 was thermodynamically
capable to reduce dioxygen, whereas 12 lies near the
reduction threshold ( E red [O 2 /O 2 •- ] = -0.59 V vs SCE).
As such, this difference likely accounts for the lower activity of 12 in this transformation.

Thus, this study showed that slight molecular
changes around the
CC triple bond can have a strong impact on reactivity due
to planarity effects. In particular, variations in the planarity exert
a direct influence on the redox properties of these materials. As
observed earlier, more planar architectures exhibit enhanced reducing
power, whereas increased torsion correlates with greater oxidative
capability. Because PAEs operate as n-type semiconductors, the reductive
strength of the CB is more consequential than the oxidative ability
of the VB, accounting for the superior catalytic activity of 11 in SET processes.

In the same vein, Zhao, Geng, and
Tan recently demonstrated that
modifying the backbone of pyrene-containing PAEs using linkers with
varying degrees of ring strain or rigidity could effectively alter
the mode of reactivity. For this, a series
of pyrene-containing PAEs interconnected with different linkers1,4-diethynylbenzene
( 15 ), 4,4′-diethynylbiphenyl ( 16 ),
and 3,4′-diethynylbiphenyl ( 17 )were synthesized
( Scheme b).

DFT calculations revealed that while compound 15 was
fully planar, compounds 16 and 17 exhibited
torsion angles of 37° and 43°, respectively. The absorption
profiles as well as the band gap were almost similar for all three
materials, enabling the study of the influence of the torsion angle
as a sole changing parameter. For this, the three materials were first
engaged in the photocatalytic oxidation of phenylboronic acid 18 to phenol 19 , known to occur through a SET
mechanism under these conditions (triethylamine being used as a sacrificial
single electron reductant). As anticipated, planar catalyst 15 delivered the highest yield of phenol 19 (99%),
whereas 16 and 17 provided only moderate
yields (43% and 36%, respectively). Interestingly, when applied to
the oxidative [4 + 2]-photocycloaddition between N -phenylmaleimide ( 20 ) and N , N -dimethylaniline ( 21 ), known to proceed via
an EnT mechanism here, the trend was reversed: photocatalyst 16 delivered the best performance, affording cycloadduct 22 in 99% yield.

The redox potentials of materials 15 - 17 were determined, revealing that
all three hPCs are thermodynamically
capable of oxidizing triethylamine ( E ox [Et 3 N/Et 3 N +• ] = +0.90 V vs
SCE) and reducing dioxygen. Consistent
with prior observations, the planar derivatives exhibited greater
single electron reducing power relative to that of the twisted analogues,
but this sole interpretation is insufficient to rationalize the pronounced
divergence observed in their photochemical reactivity. To probe the
origin of these differences, the singlet exciton binding energiesnamely
the energy required to separate the electron-hole pair into
free charge carriersof 15 , 16 , and 17 were measured to be 30, 45, and 88 meV, respectively, demonstrating
that the planar system 15 achieved more efficient charge
separation than either 16 or 17 . Fluorescence
quenching experiments in the presence of O 2 revealed a
substantial reduction in the excited-state lifetime of 15 , whereas the lifetimes of 16 and 17 remained
largely unchanged. Taken together, these data supported the conclusion
that 15 underwent productive photochemistry primarily
from its singlet excited-state via a SET pathway, facilitated by its
superior charge-separation capacity, a rare case in semiconductor
photocatalysis. In contrast, the twisted derivatives exhibited longer
triplet-state lifetimes (13, 23, and 40 μs for 15 , 16 , and 17 , respectively), consistent
with a more efficient EnT engagement. DFT calculations corroborated
this interpretation by indicating that the twisted architectures possessed
an increased propensity for intersystem crossing (ISC), thereby accounting
for their enhanced performance in the [4 + 2]-photocycloaddition.

Collectively, these studies underscored the critical influence
of the aromatic ring planarity on the photophysical and photochemical
behavior of PAEs. Enhanced planarity strengthens π conjugation,
increases visible light absorption, and promotes charge separation.
Undeniably, planar systems exhibit a higher single electron reducing
power but diminished oxidizing ability relative to their twisted counterparts.
Because PAEs behave as n-semiconductors, the reductive capability
of their CB dominates the oxidative strength of their VB in photoredox
pathways. Moreover, distortions can facilitate ISC and preferentially
channel reactivity through triplet-state EnT mechanisms. Overall,
these findings further highlight that the excited-state behavior of
PAEs can be systematically tuned through modulation of the planarity
of the aromatic substituents ( Scheme , Practical Highlight 3).

A critical
aspect to consider in the design of PAEs is the electronic influence
of their substituents. As discussed above, charge separation in PAEs
is a key determinant of the photocatalytic reactivity. While much
of the focus has been placed on enhancing conjugation to promote charge
separation, this is not the only strategy available. The incorporation
of electron-donating or electron-withdrawing monomers can generate
donor-acceptor architectures with tailored electronic properties
that facilitate exciton dissociation. So far, a few studies investigated this aspect through the prism
of organic synthesis. Unfortunately, these studies only display the
influence of electron density on resulting redox potentials, neglecting
the kinetic parameters, which might prevent the full understanding
of the structure-reactivity relationship in this section.

In 2015, the group of Zhang investigated this aspect by examining
the reactivity of single-atom-modified PAEs incorporating benzochalcogendiazole
acceptors bearing oxygen ( 23 ), sulfur ( 24 ), or selenium ( 25 ) atoms ( Scheme a). UV-vis
spectroscopy revealed that 23 and 24 exhibited
similar absorption profiles, with maxima around 800 nm, while 25 extended into the near-infrared region (>1000 nm). This
trend was reflected in their band gaps: 2.70 and 2.77 eV for 23 and 24 , respectively, and a narrower gap of
2.41 eV for 25 . Redox potential measurements indicated
that the oxygen-containing 23 had the highest single
electron reducing power (-1.22 V vs SCE), followed by 24 (-1.08 V vs SCE) and 25 (-1.06
V vs SCE), consistent with the increasing electron-accepting nature
of the chalcogen atoms. Intriguingly, 24 exhibited the
highest oxidative power (+1.69 V vs SCE), but the reason for this
remains unclear. The authors conducted EPR spectroscopy, which showed
a more intense signal for 24 , suggesting a longer-lived
exciton, although no direct lifetime measurements were reported. Then,
they tested those materials in the benchmark benzylamine 7 oxidation reaction occurring through both SET and EnT mechanisms
and found that sulfur-containing 24 outperformed its
analogues, delivering a 74% conversion of amine 7 (no
yield was provided). Although some photophysical parameters remain
unmeasured, the superior performance of 24 may result
from a more persistent excited state in the presence of a sulfur atom.

In a similar vein, the same group investigated
the donor-acceptor
effects by systematically varying the ratio of donor to acceptor in
PAEs made of triethynylbenzene as donor units and phenyl or benzobisthiadiazole
(BBT) as acceptor units. As such, they
prepared different PAEs containing 0% ( 26 ), 10% ( 27 ), 50% ( 28 ), and 100% ( 29 ) of
BBT relative to the phenyl substituentBBT being considered
as a stronger acceptor compared to phenyl ( Scheme b). As expected, increasing the proportion
of BBT in the polymer backbone led to larger red-shifts and smaller
band gaps. This trend was consistent with a stronger donor-acceptor
character: when the acceptor content increased, the energy gap between
the valence and conduction bands narrowed. The redox properties followed
the same logic. Polymers richer in BBT showed lower oxidative and
reductive potentials (in absolute terms), again reflecting the influence
of the donor-acceptor effect on the energy levels. These polymers
were then tested in the oxidative [4 + 2]-photocycloaddition between N -phenylmaleimide ( 20 ) and N , N -dimethylaniline ( 21 ), which was
found to occur through concomitant SET and EnT pathways. Interestingly,
the material containing 10% BBT ( 27 ) provided the highest
reactivity, affording cycloadduct 22 in 82% yield. Increasing
the acceptor content further proved detrimental: polymers 28 and 29 gave only 43% and 29% yield, respectively, and
the BBT-free material ( 26 ) delivered only traces of the
product. Although the exact origin of this reactivity pattern is not
yet fully understood, the authors suggested that polymer 27 strikes the best compromise between band gap and redox potential,
features that are both critical for effective visible light photocatalysis.

Altogether, these studies underscore the critical role of donor-acceptor
tuning in modulating the photophysical and photocatalytic properties
of PAEs. While increasing acceptor content generally lowers the band
gaps and redox potentials, excessive acceptor incorporation can lead
to insufficient oxidative and reducing power, ultimately diminishing
reactivity. Thus, achieving high photocatalytic performance requires
a fine balance between light absorption, exciton dissociation, and
redox potentialsideally within a band gap below 3 eV to ensure
visible light activation (Practical Highlight 4).

Because heterogeneous photocatalysis relies on interfacial
reactivity between a solid-state photocatalyst and a liquid reaction
medium, macromolecular features, particularly surface-related phenomena,
are critical for predicting the reactivity of PAEs. High porosity
in PAEs is often assumed to enhance reactivity by increasing the surface
contact with reagents, and many studies have therefore focused on
designing highly porous conjugated polymers with large specific surface
areas. However, as shown earlier, a greater
porosity does not necessarily translate into improved reactivity.
In heterogeneous synthetic photocatalysis, performance depends also
on pore accessibility since pores must be large enough to allow reagent
diffusion. A study reported by Patra
and co-workers further supports this observation ( Scheme a). They synthesized a series of porous PAEs ( 30 - 32 ) incorporating a boron dipyrromethene (BODIPY) core with
varying substituents ( R ) to vary the surface area
and pores size. The resulting materials displayed thereby a broad
range of surface areas and pore volumes: 30 ( R = n -octyl, S BET = 322 m 2 g -1 , pore diameter = 3.2 nm), 31 ( R = 4- tert -butylphenyl, S BET = 571 m 2 .g -1 , 1.4 nm), and 32 ( R = phenyl, S BET = 1010 m 2 g -1 , 0.7 nm). Despite these significant differences, all three photocatalysts
displayed nearly identical reactivity in the oxidation of thioanisole 5 , with conversion rates ranging from 91% to 96% (no yields
were given), supporting the notion that porosity is not a reliable
indicator of reactivity.

As noted above, PAEs generate electron-hole
pairs upon irradiation,
followed by charge migration to the surface, where chemical transformations
occur. However, charge delocalization in PAEs spans only ∼10
monomer unitscorresponding to ca. 0.5 nm when PPE 1 is considered as a model. Maximizing photocatalytic efficiency therefore
requires maximizing the fraction of polymer chains located within
this active surface region. Conventional PAE syntheses, however, do not permit such precise control over
polymer chain length. To address this limitation, Vilela and co-workers
prepared nanometric PAE layers by performing silica-nanoparticle-templated
Sonogashira polymerizations of 1,3,5-triethynylbenzene ( 33 ) and dibromobenzothiadiazole ( 34 ). This strategy produced silica nanoparticles uniformly coated
with a thin PAE shell. Subsequent removal of the silica core using
aqueous ammonium hydrogen difluoride yielded hollow spherical PAE
nanoparticles with diameters of 10-30 nm. Varying the amount
of silica template (mg mL -1 relative to the polymerization
medium) afforded distinct hollow spheres ( 35 - 38 ). Surface analyses revealed that higher template loadings
(higher silica concentration) produced materials with larger surface
areas and pore volumes, while the pores diameters remained essentially
constant at ∼1.4 nm ( Scheme b). The photocatalysts were assessed in the singlet
oxygen-mediated [4 + 2]-photocycloaddition of α-terpinene ( 39 ) to ascaridole ( 40 ) under flow. Polymer 38 , which possessed the largest surface area and pore volume,
delivered the highest performance with 96% conversion of 39 , and the conversion decreased with decreasing surface area. Although
the correlation was not strictly proportional, these results demonstrated
that thin-layer PAEs with higher-surface areas and pore volumes outperformed
nontemplated PAEs with lower-surface areas such as 35 .

Thus, the effect of the surface area on PAE reactivity must
be
considered with caution. While high porosity is often associated with
improved reactivity in heterogeneous photocatalysis, this relationship
is not unequivocal. The pore size and volume, in conjunction with
the particle dimensions, must be carefully optimized to facilitate
the efficient mass transport of reactants to the catalytic sites.
In this line, because charge transport in PAEs is typically confined
to approximately ten monomer units, nanometric-layer architectures
maximize the proportion of accessible active sites, thereby enhancing
reactivity (Practical Highlight 5).

PAES AS A PHOTOCATALYST FOR CONTINUOUS FLOW SYNTHESIS OF ORGANIC COMPOUNDS
The combination of photocatalysis and continuous flow processing
provides a highly efficient environment for light-driven chemistry, and the advantages become even more pronounced
when solid-state photocatalysts are employed. Flow reactors provide well-defined geometries that ensure uniform
and efficient irradiation, thereby mitigating photon attenuation and
the uneven light distribution that often limits batch photochemical
processes. In addition, the superior heat and mass transfer characteristics
of flow minimize temperature and concentration gradients, suppressing
side reactions and maintaining homogeneous reaction conditions. These
features collectively contribute to more reliable kinetics and enhance
reproducibility in photocatalytic transformations. Moreover, the small
reaction volumes and continuous throughput afford intrinsic safety
benefits, particularly when dealing with high-energy intermediates
or reactive radical species commonly accessed under photocatalytic
conditions. Embedding solid-state photocatalysts into this controlled
flow environment further strengthens these advantages: the heterogeneous
catalyst bed provides a stable and reproducible photon-catalyst
interface, avoids issues of dissolution or leaching, and enables clean
product streams without the need for catalyst separation. Solid-state
materials typically exhibit high photostability under extended illumination,
allowing long operational lifetimes and facilitating straightforward
scale translation. Together, the synergy between flow photochemistry
and heterogeneous catalytic architectures delivers a robust, safe,
and sustainable platform for advancing the practical scope of photocatalytic
synthesis.

Despite the increasing interest in photoactive polymers
for photocatalysis,
applications of PAE-based materials under continuous flow conditions
remain remarkably limited, with only a handful of examples reported
to date. The first demonstration by Vilela and co-workers, mentioned
above, employed the hollow-sphere PAE nanoparticle 38 as a heterogeneous photosensitizer within a flow photoreactor. In their study, the polymeric catalyst was dispersed
with the substrate (α-terpinene 39 , 0.1 M solution),
mixed with a continuous supply of oxygen (2 equiv), and passed through
an irradiated microstructured channel, enabling efficient generation
of singlet oxygen and achieving high substrate conversion (96%) with
short residence times (38 s, Scheme a). Although comparisons with a molecular photosensitizer
(TPP: tetraphenylporphyrin) revealed higher productivity for the homogeneous
analogue (1 mmol min -1 instead of 0.1 mmol min -1 with PAE 38 ) attributed to its intrinsically
greater singlet oxygen quantum yield, the PAE material offered compelling
practical advantages. In particular, it could be recovered by simple
filtration, exhibited excellent resistance to photodegradation and
oxidative conditions, and maintained high selectivity over multiple
reuse cycles with only modest performance decline. Further comparison
with a small-molecule analogue corresponding to the repeating unit
of the polymer confirmed that the chromophore segment within the PAE
structure was responsible for the observed photoactivity, while also
revealing that the polymeric form displayed significantly greater
photostability.

The second example was reported in 2017 by Zhang
and co-workers,
who unveiled the photocatalytic activity of PAE-coated glass fibers
integrated into a fixed-bed continuous flow photoreactor ( Scheme b). Commercial glass fibers (∼10 μm diameter)
were used as the support, and a polybenzothiadiazole coating (structurally
analogous to 38 ) was formed directly on the surface by
the Sonogashira cross-coupling polymerization of 1,3,5-triethynylbenzene
( 33 ) and 4,7-dibromo-2,1,3-benzothiadiazole ( 34 ). This procedure yielded a uniform coating of PAE (∼80 nm),
providing a surface area of 136 m 2 g -1 and a pore diameter of 1.5 nm.

The resulting photocatalyst 41 exhibited strong visible
light absorption ( abs λ tail > 700 nm),
a band gap of 2.48 eV, and redox potentials of -1.06 V vs SCE

The third and most recent example
was reported by our group in
2025. As mentioned earlier, this work
demonstrated the photocatalytic activity of the simplest PAE, poly­( para -phenylene-ethynylene) 1 , for the aerobic
oxidation of amines under both batch and continuous flow conditions
( Scheme c). For the
flow setup, photocatalyst 1 (50 mg) was premixed with
commercial glass beads (5.5 g). Electrostatic interactions between 1 and the beads ensured efficient incorporation into the packed-bed
reactor and preserved the bed homogeneity over extended operation.
Amines (0.1 M, 0.1 mL min -1 ) were delivered together
with a stream of oxygen (1.5 mL min -1 ) into a photoreactor
that was irradiated with white light. A residence time of only 9 min
was sufficient to convert a broad range of amines, including more
than 25 substrates and several natural products (such as compound 47 arising from (±)-salsolidine), into the corresponding
imines in good to excellent yields (45-100%). The high oxidation
potential of 1 (+1.67 V vs SCE) enabled the smooth oxidation
of challenging substrates such as n -hexylamine ( E ox = +1.48 V vs SCE, forming compound 48 ). Also, the flow mode consistently
outperformed batch reactions, likely due to improved mixing of the
triphasic reaction medium and superior irradiation of the catalytic
bed. Certain substrates, such as n -hexylamine and
tetrahydroisoquinoline, were successfully oxidized into compounds 48 and 49 only under flow conditions, underscoring
the decisive role of reactor design in enabling transformations inaccessible
in batch. Importantly, the single-pass flow system sustained uninterrupted
operation for over 24 h with no detectable loss of catalytic activity,
highlighting both the stability of the heterogeneous photocatalyst
and the inherent capacity of the flow setup to enable automatic catalyst
reutilization.

The continuous production of imines enabled a
telescoped two-step
sequence to access α-acylaminoamidesprivileged peptidomimetic
scaffolds via an Ugi three-component
reaction with isonitriles and carboxylic acids. Because the Ugi step required high imine concentrations
for acceptable yields, the photocatalytic module was intensified by
connecting two packed-bed reactors in series (18 min total residence
time), allowing the use of more concentrated amine solutions (0.5
M) and generating imines at 0.24 M. The crude imine stream was then
pumped into a heated coil reactor at 50 °C (50 min residence
time) together with cyclohexyl isocyanide 50 and a carboxylic
acid, affording α-acylaminoamides 51 - 53 in moderate to good overall yields (43-74%). Of
particular interest, compound 52 isolated as a
mixture of two diastereomershas been reported to exhibit antibiotic
activity against E. coli . Despite moderate
overall yields, productivity metrics highlighted the clear advantage
of flow, with 52 produced at 158 mg h -1 under continuous conditions compared to only 2.7 mg h -1 using the analogous batch protocol.

Altogether, these early examples demonstrate that the integration
of solid-state photocatalysts into continuous flow systems provides
a powerful and practical foundation for modern photocatalysis. The
precise control over photon delivery, mass transport, and thermal
management inherent to flow systems complements the robustness and
longevity of heterogeneous materials, enabling highly reproducible,
scalable, and safe photochemical processes. As illustrated by recent
advances, these combined technologies not only enhance the efficiency
of established transformations but also unlock reactivity profiles
that remain inaccessible in batch.

CONCLUSIONS AND FUTURE OUTLOOK
Over
the past few years, the photochemistry of PAE materials has
opened new opportunities for sustainable synthetic photocatalysis.
By surveying key literature precedents, we have outlined the features
that render PAEs effective in organic synthesis, with particular attention
given to the molecular and macromolecular attributes that enhance
their photocatalytic performance. A dedicated emphasis on continuous
flow systems further illustrates how this enabling technology maximizes
the efficiency, stability, and synthetic reach of PAE-based photocatalysts.

Looking forward, several directions are expected to shape the future
development of PAE-based photocatalysis: From “black-box” to mechanistic predictability.

From “black-box” to mechanistic predictability.

A central limitation of current PAE photocatalysis is
its largely
empirical nature. Future progress will require rigorous integration
of thermodynamic descriptors with kinetic analysis, supported by quantitative benchmarks such as emission quantum
yields and time-resolved spectroscopic measurements. Crucially, these photophysical parameters must be correlated
with macromolecular featuresincluding surface area, porosity,
and particle sizeto enable accurate prediction of PAE behavior
in organic synthesis and to establish a truly design-driven framework. Collectively, overcoming the prevailing “black-box”
approach will require interdisciplinary efforts that integrate synthetic
polymer chemistry, advanced spectroscopy, materials characterization,
and reaction engineering to translate structure-property relationships
into a predictive photocatalyst design. Rational molecular and macromolecular design.

Rational molecular and macromolecular design.

Advances in the PAE performance are expected to arise
from deliberate
molecular engineering aimed at improving the thermodynamic and kinetic
profiles. In particular, incorporation of donor-acceptor motifs,
heteroatom-doped backbones, or electronically polarized linkers could
expand accessible redox windows beyond current limits (≈±
2 V vs SCE), enabling more demanding oxidative and reductive transformations
and moving beyond predominantly oxygen-mediated chemistry. Reassessing photophysical models: molecular vs semiconductor
behavior.

Reassessing photophysical models: molecular vs semiconductor
behavior.

Although PAEs are often described using the semiconductor
band
theory, the finite extent of charge delocalization in many systems
raises the possibility that molecular and semiconductor behaviors
may coexist. In such cases, classical excited-state quenching pathways
may compete with or even dominate over bandlike charge transport.
Recognizing and exploiting this dual or multimodal behavioralready
suggested in related materials such as metal-organic frameworks
(MOFs) could fundamentally reshape
design principles for PAE photocatalysts. Enabling asymmetric and cooperative photocatalysis.

Enabling asymmetric and cooperative photocatalysis.

An especially compelling opportunity lies in the incorporation
of chiral motifs or ligands within PAE frameworks to enable asymmetric
photocatalysis under heterogeneous conditions. By embedding chiral
environments directly into the polymer backbone or side chains, PAEs
could support cooperative photoinduced activation and enantioselective
substrate control within a single solid material. Such an approach
would address a long-standing challenge in photocatalysis as truly
asymmetric transformations remain exceedingly rare in heterogeneous
systems. If realized, chiral PAEs could
bridge homogeneous asymmetric photocatalysis and recyclable solid-state
catalysis, opening fundamental new avenues for enantioselective synthesis. Toward predictive, multiscale, and data-driven catalyst
design.

Toward predictive, multiscale, and data-driven catalyst
design.

The convergence of density functional theory, advanced
spectroscopy,
and data-driven modeling offers a powerful opportunity to move PAE
photocatalysis from empirical optimization toward a genuinely predictive
catalyst design. Beyond idealized oligomer models, future efforts should integrate ground-state DFT and time-dependent
DFT with mesoscale descriptions to capture the effects of backbone
conformation, finite conjugation length, and interchain interactions
on excited-state energetics. When combined
with experimental benchmarks, such multiscale approaches could enable
quantitative prediction of redox potentials, triplet energies, charge
localization, and interfacial electron-transfer pathways. In parallel,
the development of structure-photophysics-reactivity
maps derived from curated experimental data sets and machine learning
tools may establish generalizable design rules for organic semiconductor
photocatalysts and guide the targeted synthesis of next-generation
PAEs. Leveraging reactor engineering as a reactivity tool.

Leveraging reactor engineering as a reactivity tool.

The compatibility of PAEs with continuous flow operation
offers
more than scalability; it provides a platform for discovering fundamentally
new reactivity. By precisely controlling photon flux, residence time,
and mass transport, flow systems can access reaction pathways and
selectivity that are unattainable in batches, offering deeper mechanistic
insight. Integrating advanced catalyst design with innovative reactor
engineering promises not only to expand the synthetic scope of PAEs
but also to establish heterogeneous flow photochemistry as a transformative
approach for sustainable and precise chemical synthesis.
