ABSTRACT
Abstract C(sp 3 )-H bonds are ubiquitous in organic molecules, and their direct functionalization has garnered extensive attention and achieved remarkable progress in recent years. The direct functionalization of simple styrene, which involves incorporating functional groups into the carbon-carbon double bond, holds particular significance for organic synthesis due to its wide-ranging applications. Radical functionalization is especially attractive as it enables the rapid and convenient transformation of simple styrene into complex molecules. Moreover, radical di-functionalization allows for the addition of various carbon-centered and even heteroatom-centered radicals to styrene. This review provides a comprehensive overview of the direct mono-functionalization of styrene and its derivatives, as well as di-functionalization reactions of styrene derivatives via radical reactions. It focuses on reaction patterns, mechanisms, and potential applications in synthetic chemistry. Special attention is given to the di-functionalization reactions of styrene derivatives with solvent molecules such as alcohols, ketones, nitriles, ethers, and thioethers, which have been named Wang reactions.

This review gives an overview of direct monofunctionalization of styrene and derivatives and di-functionalization reactions of styrene derivatives by radical reactions, with an emphasis on the reaction patterns and mechanisms.

INTRODUCTION
Styrene is one of the most valuable and readily available building blocks in natural products, pharmaceuticals and materials. As a result, many reactions involving simple styrene have been widely applied in the field of synthetic chemistry. In recent years, the functionalization of styrene and its derivatives has emerged as a powerful tool for generating highly functionalized skeletons in organic chemistry. 1 The functionalization of C(sp 3 )-H bonds has witnessed significant advances in emerging methodologies in organic chemistry, gradually evolving into a powerful tool for developing novel synthetic strategies. 2,3

The direct mono-functionalization of styrene and its derivatives has stimulated significant research activity in modern synthetic organic chemistry, with recent advances demonstrating their critical role in expanding the frontiers of reaction methodology development. This transformative approach has garnered considerable attention due to its capacity for constructing complex architectures while maintaining atomic economy and operational simplicity. This review systematically examines various catalytic methodologies, including multi-component reactions, photochemical reactions, Markovnikov/anti-Markovnikov additions, and [4 + 2] cycloadditions, which collectively illustrate the versatility of styrene derivatives as synthetic intermediates.

The direct di-functionalization of styrene derivatives has also emerged as a pivotal research frontier in synthetic organic chemistry, driven by its capacity to enable the simultaneous incorporation of two functional groups into the carbon-carbon double bond through a single synthetic operation. This transformation holds particular significance for constructing complex molecular architectures while maintaining operational simplicity and atom economy. Among the established methodologies, radical di-functionalization and metal-catalyzed nucleophilic di-functionalization has gained prominence due to its exceptional synthetic versatility. Then, from the synthetic perspective, radical di-functionalization being more appealing as it allows the addition of simple styrene by various carbon-centered radicals and even heteroatom-centered radicals. In addition to covering the direct mono-functionalization of styrene and its derivatives in part 1, this review also presents an overview of the radical di-functionalization of simple styrene, focusing on the reaction patterns and mechanisms, as the di-functionalization of unactivated styrene has emerged as a powerful tool for constructing highly functionalized framework molecules in organic synthesis. 4-7

DIRECT MONO-FUNCTIONALIZATION OF STYRENE AND DERIVATIVES
Transition-metal-catalyzed transfer hydrocyanation employing safe HCN precursors has been developed, although these reagents typically require Lewis acid activation; Studer and coworkers reported a nickel-catalyzed Markovnikov transfer hydrocyanation proceeding without Lewis acid, 8 utilizing readily prepared pro-aromatic 1-isopropylcyclohexa-2,5-diene-1-carbonitrile as the HCN source, demonstrating broad substrate scope and high functional group tolerance with terminal styrene derivatives, dienes and internal alkynes converted in good to excellent selectivities ( Scheme 1 ). 8

Reactions proceed under comparably mild conditions at 90 °C, with styrene derivatives exhibiting excellent Markovnikov selectivity, while mechanistic studies offer insights into the origin of the regioselectivity ( Scheme 2 ). 8

In 2023, Zhang and co-workers reported a regioselective hydroboration of styrene with high turnover frequencies; homoleptic complexes with octahedral coordination modes generally exhibit lower catalytic activity due to saturated metal center coordination that inhibits substrate activation, whereas Zhang demonstrated that a homoleptic octahedral cobalt complex featuring 4′-pyridyl-2,2′;6′,2′;6′,2′′-terpyridine, which undergoes mono-protonation at the non-coordinating pyridyl moiety upon crystallization, functions as an efficient precatalyst for the Markovnikov-selective hydroboration of styrene derivatives and exhibits promising potential for related hydro-functionalization reactions ( Scheme 3 ). 9

The base-catalyzed addition of 1-cyclopropylethanol to styrene derivatives followed by acidic workup enables anti-Markovnikov hydration, as demonstrated by Bandar and coworkers who developed a base-catalyzed approach for this transformation. The method's utility is evidenced by multigram-scale reactions and its application in a two-step hydration/cyclization process of ortho -halogenated styrenes to synthesize 2,3-dihydrobenzofuran derivatives, with the base-catalyzed addition of 1-cyclopropylethanol providing a convenient route to β-aryl substituted alcohols, while catalytic P 4 - t Bu superbase affords superior yields compared to inorganic bases, though KO- t -Bu/18-crown-6 may represent a more practical catalyst system for large-scale hydration ( Scheme 4 ). 10

The de-aromative cycloaddition reaction provides a strategic approach for constructing C(sp 3 )-rich three-dimensional molecular architectures from planar aromatic compounds, yet the process remains challenging due to significant reactivity and selectivity barriers. Maji and co-workers addressed this challenge by reporting an intermolecular de-aromative [4 + 2] cycloaddition of naphthalenes via visible-light energy-transfer catalysis, which enables the conversion of structurally diverse 2-acyl naphthalenes and styrenes-tolerating a wide range of functional groups-into a variety of bicyclo[2.2.2]octa-2,5-diene scaffolds in high yields. The efficacy of the introduced methods was further demonstrated through post-synthetic diversification of the products, with the reaction scalable and compatible with late-stage modification of various complex molecules, while photoluminescence, electrochemical, kinetic, and control experiments provided insights into the characteristics of this visible-light energy-transfer catalysis. 11 Glorius and co-workers developed an energy transfer-enabled, highly regio- and diastereoselective intermolecular [4 + 2] dearomative cycloaddition reaction via visible-light photocatalysis. By employing an iridium photosensitizer, inert bicyclic azaarenes can be excited to their triplet state to disrupt aromaticity, thereby realizing Diels-Alder-type cycloaddition with electronically diverse alkenes. Notably, the transformation selectively occurs at the carbon sites outside the nitrogen-containing aromatic ring. This strategy effectively overcomes the intrinsic reactivity and selectivity limitations of conventional aromatic cycloaddition, efficiently constructing structurally complex bridged polycyclic skeletons that are difficult to synthesize via traditional methods ( Scheme 5 ). 11

Ye group reported an electrochemical [4 + 2] annulation-rearrangement-aromatization of styrenes, representing an electrochemical strategy for synthesizing functionalized naphthalene derivatives through [4 + 2] annulation, rearrangement, and aromatization under mild conditions with optimal atom and step economy. The dehydrodimer generated via [4 + 2] cycloaddition of 4-methoxy-α-methylstyrene was isolated and identified as the key intermediate, which undergoes rearrangement-aromatization to yield the final products, with the reaction exhibiting notable scalability and significant synthetic utility for constructing polycyclic aromatic compounds ( Scheme 6 ). 12

Mechanistic studies indicate that the dehydrodimer serves as the key intermediate in this process, with the proposed mechanism for the electrochemical [4 + 2] annulation-rearrangement-aromatization illustrated in Scheme 7 . 12

Compared with the classic photochemical cycloaddition pathways of styrene derivatives, electrochemical synthesis has become a sustainable and efficient tool for radical-mediated alkene functionalization and cycloaddition transformations. A series of representative frontier studies have greatly diversified the reaction types and mechanistic pathways of alkene difunctionalization and cyclization. Samanta et al. reported an electrochemically driven formal (3 + 2) cycloaddition reaction of unactivated alkenes with β-dicarbonyl compounds. This strategy overcomes the intrinsic reactivity limitations of inert olefins and enables the efficient construction of functionalized dihydrofuran skeletons under mild and oxidant-free electrochemical conditions. 12 Lin and co-workers developed a manganese-catalyzed electrochemical diazidation of various alkenes. By virtue of metal-mediated azidyl radical transfer, this approach achieves efficient vicinal dinitrogen functionalization of alkenes with broad substrate scope and excellent functional group compatibility, providing a facile synthetic route for high-value diamine derivatives. 12 In addition, Lin's systematic electrocatalytic investigations on radical alkene difunctionalization further clarified the synergistic mechanism between transition metal catalysis and electrochemical oxidation, and revealed the universal principle of electrogenerated radical intermediates in governing alkene addition and cyclization processes. 12 Collectively, these electrochemical radical strategies serve as important supplements to the conventional photochemical [4 + 2] cycloaddition systems of styrene derivatives, further expanding the synthetic methodology for controllable radical cyclization and difunctionalization of alkene substrates.

Loh and co-workers reported a copper-catalyzed trifluoromethylation of styrene derivatives, developing a protocol using CF 3 SO 2 Na as the trifluoromethylating source in the presence of t BuOO t Bu, whereby various styrene derivatives were demonstrated to be suitable substrates under mild reaction conditions, leading to the corresponding trifluoromethylated styrenes in good yields. Mechanistic studies suggest the reaction proceeds via a radical pathway, providing a general and straightforward approach to trifluoromethylated styrene derivatives with excellent stereoselectivity for styrene substrates ( Scheme 8 and Scheme 9 ). 13

In 2020, Novák and co-workers reported a photocatalytic palladium-catalyzed fluoroalkylation of styrene derivatives, developing a visible light-induced palladium catalyzed fluoroalkylation method that enables the introduction of trifluoroethyl, difluoroethyl and other fluoroalkyl fragments into styrenes under mild reaction conditions with good yields through a Heck-type alkyl coupling reaction. And then, successfully using five different fluoroalkyl iodides, and offering an efficient disconnection for incorporating fluorine-containing functional groups into styrene derivatives ( Scheme 10 ). 14

In 2020, Duan and co-workers reported a visible-light induced anti-Markovnikov hydrosulfonation reaction of styrene derivatives with sodium sulfinates under mild conditions, demonstrating good functional-group tolerance in both styrenes and sodium sulfinates to yield various sulfones in moderate to good yields with high regioselectivity, where mechanistic investigations revealed the involvement of a visible-light induced radical process, representing the first example of the hydrosulfonation reaction of styrene derivatives ( Scheme 11 ). 15 The plausible mechanism of the hydrosulfonation process was proposed by Duan and co-workers in Scheme 12 .

Fleischer and co-workers reported a palladium-catalyzed thiocarbonylation of styrene derivatives, where the combination of thiols as nucleophiles and a bidentate ligand ensures a unique reaction outcome with high regioselectivity. The mild reaction conditions (ambient temperature, low catalyst loading) and employment of a CO surrogate render this transformation a practical method for synthesizing thioesters from readily available feedstocks, with the devised catalytic system tolerating a wide range of functional groups for the thiocarbonylation of substituted styrenes, representing a three-component reaction involving styrene, carbon monoxide, and thiols ( Scheme 13 ). 16

Fleischer and coworkers proposed a catalytic cycle based on the established hydride mechanism for the carbonylation reaction, with the key distinction that the thiol functions as a ligand ( Scheme 14 ). 16

In 2024, Yang and Wu reported the utilization of cesium salt derivatives of tertiary alcohols as precursors to alkyl radicals in a three-component reaction with styrene and aromatic acyl fluorides for the synthesis of α-arylalkyl aryl ketones. Wherein the aroyl fluoride reacts with the NHC catalyst to form an acyl azolium ion, which is subsequently reduced by the photoredox catalyst to generate the corresponding ketyl radical anion, while the C-radical derived from oxalate oxidation adds to the styrene derivative, followed by cross-coupling of the resulting addition radical with the ketone radical fragmented by NHC to afford the target product ( Scheme 15 ). 17

The key step in this reaction involves the reduction of an acyl azolium intermediate to generate a stabilized azolium radical species, which subsequently participates in radical-radical coupling with tertiary alkyl radicals. This methodology enables rapid access to diverse γ-tertiary alkyl ketones while maintaining excellent functional group compatibility. Furthermore, the use of various substituted styrenes facilitates the construction of quaternary carbon centers adjacent to the carbonyl group.

A reaction between an aroyl fluoride and the NHC catalyst yields an acyl azolium ion, which can be reduced to the corresponding ketyl radical anion. Saito and Mori reported a C(sp 3 )-H bond functionalization strategy with styrenes via hydrogen-atom transfer to an aqueous hydroxyl radical under photocatalytic conditions. This method enables redox-neutral addition of α-C-H bonds from acetonitrile and acetone to styrenes through hydrogen-atom transfer from relatively acidic and water-miscible C(sp 3 )-H bonds to an aqueous hydroxyl radical, which is generated iteratively via water oxidation catalyzed by silver-nanoparticle-loaded titania. Jamison and co-workers pioneered a direct β-selective hydrocarboxylation protocol for styrenes utilizing continuous flow photoredox catalysis with CO 2 . This methodology exhibited exceptional versatility in accommodating diverse functionalized terminal styrenes, including α- and β-substituted analogs, while demonstrating robust compatibility with styrene derivatives bearing varied functional group architectures ( Scheme 21 ). 20

Suero and co-workers pioneered a transition-metal-free and diazo-free protocol for styrene cyclopropanation, where visible-light induced photoinduced generation of iodomethyl carbonyl radicals enabled cyclopropanation of diverse styrene derivatives with exceptional chemoselectivity and functional group tolerance. This methodology demonstrated remarkable versatility in functionalizing complex styrene architectures and biomolecules through selective radical trapping mechanisms ( Scheme 22 ). 21

In 2020, Gunnoe and co-workers reported a rhodium-catalyzed oxidative arene alkenylation protocol for synthesizing stilbene and its derivatives from arenes and styrenes, achieving good to high yields with predictable regioselectivity. This transition metal-mediated methodology provides an alternative strategy for arene functionalization through C-H activation and olefin insertion, eliminating the requirement for prefunctionalization via weaker C-X bonds (X = halide or pseudohalide) or directing groups. The reaction demonstrates exceptional compatibility with diverse functional groups on both arene ( e.g. , fluorine, chlorine, trifluoromethyl, ester, nitro, acetate, cyano, and ether functionalities) and olefin substrates. Notably, this approach enables scalable synthesis of medicinally relevant stilbenes while circumventing traditional limitations associated with acid-catalyzed alkylation and palladium-coupling methodologies ( Scheme 24 ). 22

Gunnoe and co-workers proposed a strategy involving rhodium-mediated C-H activation of arenes followed by vinyl arene insertion and β-hydride elimination to enable oxidative arene alkenylation with styrenes. This process utilized a rhodium catalyst precursor and an air-recyclable copper( ii ) oxidant, establishing a catalytic cycle for late transition metal-catalyzed oxidative arene alkenylation to produce stilbene and its derivatives ( Scheme 25 ). 22

Bower and co-workers demonstrated an iridium-catalyzed α-selective arylation of styrenes through dual C-H functionalization, employing an Ir( i )-system modified with a ferrocene-derived bisphosphine ligand. This methodology provides a regioisomeric alternative to the Pd-catalyzed Fujiwara-Moritani reaction, enabling selective C-H activation at the α-position of styrenes. Notably, the approach expands the synthetic utility of C-H functionalization strategies by accommodating diverse styrene derivatives while maintaining excellent chemoselectivity and functional group compatibility ( Scheme 26 ). 23

DI-FUNCTIONALIZATION REACTIONS OF STYRENE DERIVATIVES
Miura and co-workers pioneered a three-component 1,2-acylcyanation protocol for styrenes in 2025, leveraging photoinduced nickel catalysis to generate acyl radicals from nickel( ii ) acyl complexes formed between nickel(0) precursors and acyl fluorides via ligand-to-metal charge transfer (LMCT). This mild transformation enables late-stage functionalization (LSF) of complex natural product derivatives while maintaining broad substrate compatibility, including sensitive bioactive molecules. The methodology demonstrates exceptional versatility in difunctionalizing styrenic systems through selective radical trapping mechanisms, establishing nickel-catalyzed photoredox processes as transformative tools for synthetic organic chemistry ( Scheme 27 ). 24

Experimental and computational studies suggest that acyl radicals are generated via photoinduced LMCT in nickel( ii ) acyl fluoride complexes. This mechanistic pathway is illustrated in Scheme 28 . The reaction initiates with oxidative addition of benzoyl fluoride to a nickel(0) complex, forming nickel( ii ) acyl fluoride complex I. Subsequent photoexcitation induces LMCT, producing a nickel( i ) fluoride complex (II) and benzoyl radical. The benzoyl radical then undergoes addition to 4-cyanostyrene, generating a benzylic radical intermediate (III). This intermediate is intercepted by nickel( i ) cyanide complex IV, formed through transmetalation between complex II and TMSCN. Finally, reductive elimination from nickel( ii ) alkyl cyanide complex V yields the difunctionalized product 35 while regenerating the nickel(0) catalyst. 24

Lian and co-workers pioneered a copper-catalyzed chloro-arylsulfonylation methodology for styrene derivatives, utilizing sulfur dioxide insertion to construct C-S bonds. This protocol exhibits broad functional group compatibility, mild reaction conditions, and excellent regioselectivity. Notably, the method demonstrates exceptional robustness through successful gram-scale synthesis while maintaining product integrity, underscoring its potential for industrial applications ( Scheme 29 ). 25

Qiu and co-workers developed an electroreductive arylcarboxylation protocol for styrenes in 2024, achieving three-component coupling of styrenes, aryl halides, and CO 2 through organo-mediated radical pathways. β-Amidated carboxylic acids or succinimide derivatives represent a versatile chemical scaffold widely utilized in pharmaceutical, agrochemical, and polymer science applications. In 2024, König and co-workers pioneered a visible-light driven protocol for synthesizing 1,2-dicarboxyl compounds through radical coupling of styrene, CO 2 , and 1,4-carbamoyl-dihydropyridine. This methodology exhibits a broad substrate scope under mild reaction conditions, including late stage functionalization. This method demonstrates excellent regioselectivity, enabling the synthesis of functionalized molecules in a single step directly from cost-effective, readily available starting materials ( Scheme 35 ). 28

In 2015, Wang and co-workers reported a copper/manganese-cocatalyzed di-functionalization of styrene derivatives with ketones. This reaction proceeded via free-radical addition of terminal vinylarenes, yielding 1,4-dicarbonyl products with excellent regioselectivity and efficiency in a single step. The method provides a straightforward approach for synthesizing functionalized molecules using inexpensive reactants. Mechanistic investigations confirmed the involvement of a radical pathway ( Scheme 37 ). 29

Wang and co-workers reported a metal-free di-functionalization strategy for styrenes with alkyl nitriles in 2016, providing γ-ketonitriles through radical coupling. This reaction delivered γ-ketonitriles in moderate to good yields with broad functional group compatibility, featuring operational simplicity and atom- and step-economic design under mild conditions ( Scheme 38 ). 30

A preliminary radical pathway was established through alkyl radical trapping experiments, with conjugated systems demonstrating enhanced stabilization of radical intermediates. Non-conjugated olefins exhibited negligible reactivity under these conditions. The phenyl group facilitated radical stabilization via resonance donation of π-electrons to the nascent radical intermediate through p-π conjugation. Intermolecular kinetic isotope effect (KIE) were observed, with a KH/KD value of 15.7 confirming the hydrogen abstraction step as the rate-determining process ( Scheme 39 ). 30

Wang and co-workers established a copper-catalyzed di-functionalization methodology in 2019, using Cu(OAc) 2 and additives to mediate styrene-ether cross-coupling. This protocol demonstrated efficient access to functionalized products through radical intermediates, featuring operational simplicity and broad functional group compatibility with easily accessible starting materials under mild conditions. The reaction exhibited moderate to good yields across diverse styrene and ether substrates, confirming its practical utility in synthetic chemistry. 31 DFT simulation and theory calculation provided important evidence for the proposed free radical mechanism ( Scheme 40 ). 31

In 2020, Wang and co-workers extended their prior investigations on styrene di-functionalization by exploring new reaction paradigms. A series of coupling agents-including N -methylpyrrolidine, toluene, dimethyl carbonate, dimethyl sulfate, nitromethane, and dichloroethane were systematically evaluated, though none facilitated the desired transformations. Notably, shifting focus to thioether systems enabled metal-free di-functionalization with moderate product yields ( Scheme 41 ). 32

Wang and co-workers investigated the radical mechanism underlying styrene di-functionalization with thioethers as the example ( Scheme 42 ). First, radical A ( tert -butyl-O· or ·OH) was generated from TBHP and passed the single electron to tetrahydrothiophene, then tetrahydrothiophene radical B was formed. Second, tetrahydrothiophene radical B transferred the single electron to styrene, generating a stable and persistent radical C with a single electron at the benzyl position of styrene. The benzylic radical C could be stabilized through big π system of the benzene ring and radical p-π conjugate effect. The conjugation effect makes it difficult for the free radical C to quench or self-couple, and it is easy to accumulate to a sufficient reaction concentration, thereby effectively capturing the transient reactive radicals ( tert -butyl-O-O· and ·OH) in the reaction system to form the final product. The formation of the persistent radical C is the key to the success in this reaction. 32

This sequential detection confirmed the preferential generation of tetrahydrothiophene radical B under the reaction conditions. The persistent radical C ( Scheme 42 ) is continuously formed after radical B is generated. The progressive accumulation of radical C ( Scheme 42 ) ultimately triggers subsequent transformation processes. As evidenced by adducts D and E ( Scheme 43 ), radical B ( Scheme 42 ) undergoes single-electron transfer to styrene, initiating a radical relay mechanism that generates stabilized benzyl radical C through conjugated π-system stabilization.

The ether-indene coupling reaction was first reported by Wang's group, where indene is structurally analogous to styrene linked via a methylene bridge ( Scheme 44a ). This transformation is anticipated to yield two final products (F and G) through radical-mediated mechanisms. However, only product F was isolated. Product F formed via path I proceeded through intermediate F′, whose unpaired electron was stabilized by the extended π-conjugated system of the benzene ring. Conversely, formation of product G would involve intermediate G′, but its unpaired electron could not be stabilized by the benzene ring. Wang's group verified in 2016 that non-conjugated styrene analogs ( e.g. , allylbenzene and vinyl cyclohexane) did not react with acetonitrile ( Scheme 44b ).

Wang and co-workers’ methodology consistently delivered products bearing an α-carbonyl group on the benzene ring. 28-32 However, Li introduced a tert -butylperoxyl group at the α-position of the benzene ring using dibenzoyl methane as the attacking reagent ( Scheme 45a ), 33 while Zhu et al. installed a hydroxyl group at the α-position via 1 H -benzotriazole as the attacking reagent ( Scheme 45b ). 34 Their experimental outcomes were further validated in Wang's laboratory.

The products obtained by Li and Zhu do not represent the terminal species as under Wang's reaction conditions. A pivotal role was played by DBU in Wang's methodology. In the case of the α- tert -butylperoxyl-substituted product ( Scheme 45a , Li's product), DBU abstracts the acidic α-hydrogen atom of the peroxide to generate a transient carbanion intermediate, which undergoes a concerted deprotonation-rearrangement sequence to yield the α-carbonyl product ( Scheme 46 , path I) accompanied by the elimination of the tert -butoxyl anion (Kornblum-DeLaMare rearrangement). 35 DBU is consistently present in the reaction systems developed by Wang's group, exclusively yielding α-carbonyl products. In contrast, the α-hydroxyl-substituted derivative ( Scheme 45b , Zhu's product) undergoes TBHP-mediated oxidation to generate the α-carbonyl product ( Scheme 46 , path II), where the benzene α-hydroxyl group is selectively oxidized to an α-carbonyl functionality under these conditions. 36

Wang's group established the universal mechanism for the Wang's reaction. The process initiates with the thermal decomposition of TBHP into tert -butoxyl and hydroxyl radical. Subsequently, these radicals activate TBHP to generate a tert -butylperoxyl radical. This peroxyl radical then abstracts hydrogen atom from protic solvents ( e.g. , alcohols, ketones, nitriles, ethers, and thioethers) through hydrogen abstraction, forming radical intermediate (L) stabilized by conjugating with the aromatic ring. Third, radical L attacked styrene and producing an intermediate M. Subsequently, intermediate M engaged in radical coupling with either hydroxyl or tert -butylperoxyl radical to yield intermediates N or O, respectively. Ultimately, DBU-mediated oxidation of intermediate N by TBHP 36 and Kornblum-DeLaMare rearrangement 35 of intermediate O under identical conditions converged to afford the same α-carbonyl product ( Scheme 47 ).

The significance of the Wang reaction lies in its capacity to overcome the high dissociation energy of C(sp 3 )-H bonds, enabling the valorization of readily available hydrocarbons like styrene into high-value fine chemicals and pharmaceutical intermediates under mild conditions. Notably, this transformation achieves γ-ketone nitrile di-functionalization through single-step radical cascade processes utilizing styrene derivatives, a methodology formally termed the Wang reaction. 37,38 The reaction mechanism has been systematically elucidated and generalized as a paradigm for radical-mediated C-H functionalization ( Scheme 48 ). 32

Zeitler and co-workers established a photoredox-catalyzed C-C cross-coupling methodology for styrenes, employing readily accessible α-carbonyl acetates as convenient alkyl radical precursors to enable efficient photocatalytic cross-coupling with diverse styrene derivatives. This umpolung-type transformation critically relies on the application of fac-Ir(ppy) 3 as a photocatalyst to enforce oxidative quenching cycles ( Scheme 49 ). 39

Mechanistic details of this dual catalytic coupling reaction were elucidated through comprehensive Stern-Volmer and cyclic voltammetry analyses. The water-assisted Lewis acid/photoredox catalytic activation strategy enables stoichiometric radical cross-coupling between α-acetoxy carbonyl compounds and styrenes, followed by selective Markovnikov-type hydroxylation to afford versatile 1,4-difunctionalized products in high yields ( Scheme 50 ). 39

In 2025, Wu and co-workers established a generalized photoredox-catalyzed methodology for β-hydroxy acid derivative synthesis from styrene derivatives. The reaction proceeds via a concerted radical-triggered alkoxycarbonylation mechanism, where the photocatalyst facilitates oxidative quenching cycles to enable selective C-H functionalization. This strategy employs a one-pot, multicomponent cascade integrating radical-mediated alkoxycarbonylation with nucleophilic addition, achieving high functionalization efficiency under mild conditions ( Scheme 51 ). 40

Mechanistic investigations employing radical trapping experiments, luminescence quenching analyses, and cyclic voltammetry demonstrate consistency with alkoxycarbonyl radical-mediated addition, supporting an oxidative quenching mechanism for the photocatalyst. Alkenedifunctionalization represents a highly attractive strategy in synthetic organic chemistry. In 2024, Granados and co-workers pioneered a photoredox-catalyzed di-functionalization methodology for styrene derivatives, enabling the synthesis of 2-hydroxytrifluoroethylacetophenones. This light-mediated transformation employs an organophotoredox system to generate a 1-hydroxy-2,2,2-trifluoroethyl carbon-centered radical as the key synthetic intermediate, which undergoes sequential Giese addition with styrenes followed by Kornblum oxidation. The protocol demonstrates exceptional mildness and cost-effectiveness, using an organic photocatalyst and DMSO as the terminal oxidant. 41

This di-functionalization process capitalizes on DMSO-mediated generation of the critical α-hydroxy-α-trifluoroethyl carbon-centered radical. The protocol employs the organic photoredox catalyst 4DPAIPN under mild oxidation conditions to enable rapid access to 2-hydroxytrifluoroethylacetophenones. The synthetic methodology demonstrates broad applicability for di-functionalizing both simple and complex styrene derivatives, including structurally challenging heteroaromatic systems ( Scheme 53 ). 41

Mechanistic investigations are consistent with a net-neutral radical/polar crossover photoredox mechanism. A plausible mechanistic pathway for organophotoredox-mediated difunctionalization of styrene is illustrated in Scheme 54 . 41

In 2023, Singh and co-workers pioneered a photoredox-catalyzed intermolecular alkylcarbofunctionalization strategy for styrene derivatives. This methodology leverages α-aminoalkyl radical-mediated halogen atom abstraction to generate reactive α-haloalkyl radicals, which subsequently engage styrene derivatives as (poly)haloalkyl radical acceptors. The protocol facilitates sequential radical addition and C-C bond formation with quinoxalinones. The efficacy of radical species generated through halogen atom transfer in mediating intermolecular transformations was demonstrated. This methodology employed diverse, commercially accessible halogenated precursors to enable access to unexplored halogenated chemical space. The reaction proceeded under mild conditions, exhibited broad substrate scope, and was scalable to gram-scale synthesis ( Scheme 55 ). 42

Singh and co-workers reported a proposed mechanistic pathway ( Scheme 56 ) involving photoexcited catalyst-mediated radical generation. The process initiates with reductive quenching of the excited photocatalyst by DIPEA, yielding an amine radical cation B and a Ru( i ) species. Subsequent deprotonation of radical B by DIPEA generates an α-amino radical D, which abstracts a halogen atom from a halogenated precursor to form a halomethyl radical F. This halomethyl radical undergoes styrene addition to produce a benzylic radical G, which is stabilized by quinoxalinone trapping to form an N -centered radical H. Transposition of H to a carbon-centered radical I, followed by oxidation and elimination steps, ultimately delivers the final product. 42

CONCLUSIONS
C(sp 3 )-H bonds are the most common structures in organic molecules. In recent years, direct functionalization of C(sp 3 )-H bonds has attracted wide attention and made significant progress. This review summarized direct mono-functionalization of styrene derivatives and di-functionalization reactions of styrene derivatives by radical reaction, and special comment on the di-functionalization reactions of styrene derivatives with solvent molecules (Wang reaction) with an especially emphasis on the reaction mechanisms. In the Wang reaction, heteroatom-containing substrates including alcohols, ketones, nitriles, ethers, and thioethers exhibit excellent reactivity, which originates from the electronic regulation of internal oxygen, nitrogen, and sulfur heteroatoms that activate adjacent C(sp 3 )-H bonds and stabilize radical intermediates. Transition metal catalysts play a pivotal synergistic role in this reaction by activating C(sp 3 )-H bonds, stabilizing radical species, and lowering the energy barrier of key transition states, thereby enabling efficient radical relay difunctionalization. Although remarkable progress has been made in this area, several challenges and open questions remain at the forefront of current research, most reported functionalization reactions of styrene derivatives require relatively high catalyst loadings, and high reaction temperatures, and the use of powerful oxidizing agents often limit the scalability and practical application of these transformations. In some cases, low product yields or poor functional group tolerance continue to hinder broader industrial applications. To date, enantioselective functionalization of styrene derivatives remains limited due to the challenges associated with controlling the stereochemistry of reactive and unstable radical intermediates.
